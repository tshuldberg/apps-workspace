Our number one rule: the app never lies.
If a message did not really send, the app must not show "Sent." If a feature is not real yet, we show an honest empty screen, never a fake one.

🦫 Meerkat the private clubhouse network

Like Discord, but nobody's messages sit on a big company's computer. Phones pass sealed envelopes to each other; the "post office" computers that help carry them cannot open them.

💬 Private messages (1-on-1 and groups)
Built real direct messages. Every note is sealed so only the right person can open it.
WHY The app had group chat but no way to pass a private note to one friend. It was the biggest missing piece before charging money for the app.
✅ Honest checkmarks
"Delivered" and "Read" only light up when the other phone sends back a signed, unfakeable receipt.
WHY Most apps guess. Guessing is a small lie, and our rule is no lies.
🔐 New locks when someone leaves
Kick someone from a group and everyone remaining gets a fresh secret code. A test with three pretend phones proves the removed phone cannot read anything new.
WHY Leaving a group should mean actually losing the key, not just being hidden from the list.
🗑️ Delete-for-everyone, safely
You can erase your own message from everyone's phone. Two separate guards check the deleter is the writer.
WHY People make mistakes and should be able to unsend, but nobody may erase someone else's words.
🚪 Really removing a member
The clubhouse owner signs a removal order with unforgeable math; all phones obey and the locks rotate.
WHY The old remove button did not truly work all the way down. A safety button that only pretends is worse than no button.
💅 Chat that feels like chat
Message bubbles, emoji reactions, replies, @mentions, and new messages arriving by themselves.
WHY The engine was great but the screen looked like a science experiment. Judges scored it 4.5/10 next to Discord. People will not use a clubhouse that feels like homework.
🎟️ Join with a link or QR code
Tap a link or scan a code, see a preview card, tap Join. Five clean tabs instead of an admin maze.
WHY Joining used to mean pasting a long weird code. Every extra step loses half the new members.
📰 A feed that shows posts
Cards with pictures, reply counts, reactions, and real profile photos. Explanations moved behind an info button.
WHY The old feed mostly explained how it works, like a TV showing its own wiring diagram. Judges scored it 3/10 next to Reddit.
🔗 Link previews that protect you
The SENDER's phone fetches the preview and ships it with the link. Your phone never visits the site on its own.
WHY Nobody should be able to make YOUR phone knock on a stranger's door just by sending you a link.
🛡️ Armor for helper computers
Size limits and junk limits on the volunteer computers that store clubhouse data.
WHY Without limits, a prankster could stuff the mailbox with pizza flyers until it bursts.
🧍 "Prove you are a person" gate
A humanity check for public spaces that fails closed: if the check cannot run, the door stays shut.
WHY Public areas attract robot spam. And a broken lock should be a locked door, not an open one.
📡 Communities choose how data travels
A clubhouse can demand "local Wi-Fi only, never the internet," and every part of the app obeys, even refusing updates that arrive the wrong way.
WHY Some groups (a family, a classroom) want their stuff to physically never leave the building. Now that promise is enforced by code, not by trust.
🤝 Auto-connect
Phones find each other and sync on their own, with polite retry timers.
WHY Normal people will not remember to press a Sync button. The magic has to happen by itself.
🎬 Your clubhouse becomes a private Netflix
Shelves for videos, music, and photos inside a clubhouse: browse, play, photo timeline, offline maps, themes and templates. Each file has its own tiny lock, wrapped by the clubhouse key. Playback runs through a tiny server living INSIDE the app.
WHY Every clubhouse was already a locked storage box; it just had no shelves or play button. Families get a private Netflix + Spotify + photo album with no company in the middle.
💵 Price locked
Exactly two prices, decided by the founder: $4.99 once for the app, and an optional $4.99 per month for hosted storage. A test now guards the price so it cannot drift.
WHY A draft document briefly invented other prices. Now the code itself remembers the real ones.
🔍 What the inspectors caught here

1) If the "you are removed" letter arrives through the backup gossip path instead of the normal mailbox, the phone forgets to cross the removed person off its member list (they still cannot read new secrets, but wrong lists cause trouble). 2) One helper computer saves a progress note in a way that could tear in half if power dies mid-write. 3) The chat bubble can say "sent" even if the mailbox hand-off quietly failed. All on the fix-it list.

👨‍🍳 BestChef the cooking contest app

Cook a dish, film it, upload it, get votes. Goal: launch around the world in 7+ languages. This week: speak every language properly, and make cheating impossible.

🌍 21 languages
Nearly 1,000 phrases translated into 21 language files, including the legal text. Servers now send error CODES, and your phone translates them.
WHY Before, the server scolded everyone in English no matter what language they spoke. A world launch where the app half-speaks your language is embarrassing.
↔️ Right-to-left, plurals, times
Mirrored icons for Arabic and Hebrew, official counting rules for languages like Polish and Russian, local time formats, and multilingual emoji keyword maps.
WHY "1 vote / 2 votes" is easy in English and wrong in half the world. Details like this are the difference between translated and actually local.
🍜 Dish names in your language
Dishes carry translations and search works in your language. Non-Latin dish names no longer break dish creation.
WHY A cooking app where you cannot type your own food's real name would be a joke in every country we want to launch in.
🧑‍⚖️ A referee desk
A separate app for human moderators: reports, appeals, proof photo queues, promoting winning videos to the public feed, and auto-restoring videos that win appeals.
WHY A public contest without referees turns into chaos in a week. And the law (like the EU's DSA) requires a real appeal process.
🚫 Anti-cheating armor
Action limits per person, a permanent fingerprint list of every vote-proof photo, and server-enforced blocks.
WHY Before, you could delete your vote and vote again with the same photo, forever. Contests only matter if cheating is hard.
📤 Uploads that survive bad Wi-Fi
A sturdy waiting line on the phone keeps your video safe through tunnels and dead batteries, plus a cleanup robot for leftover files.
WHY Losing someone's cooking video because the elevator ate the signal is how you lose that user forever.
📜 The law stuff
Per-country age gates and a full legal corpus in the app.
WHY Different countries have different rules. Getting this wrong gets an app banned.
🏆 Fun stuff
Video comments, bookmarks that sync across devices, confetti trophy moments.
WHY People stay for the fun. Two of these were promised tickets that had quietly turned into "coming soon" stubs, which breaks the honesty rule, so we built them for real.
🔍 What the inspectors caught here

1) The robot judge that screens vote photos is still a PRETEND judge with stand-in scores; the real photo-checking service is not hired yet. The contest must not open publicly until it is. 2) Two copies of the checking robot can grab the same photo at once and both stamp it; the desk needs a take-a-number system. 3) A new health-report update accidentally erased some warning lights from the operators' dashboard.

🏋️ DoWork the gym coach app

Built around one real personal trainer: his video library, his students, private coaching, and hands-free voice control while you sweat.

🎥 The trainer's studio
Profile page, video upload and management, and QR invite codes that turn people into his students.
WHY The whole business idea: the trainer's first user IS a real trainer. He needs to run his business inside the app on day one.
📝 Private coaching loop
Students send form-check videos; the trainer replies with feedback pinned to exact seconds ("at 0:32 your back is bending").
WHY That is what people pay a trainer for: not videos, but "watch ME and fix ME."
🎙️ Voice control
Say "pause," "next," "repeat" mid-exercise. Listening happens ON the phone only.
WHY Your hands are holding weights. And your voice should not be shipped to a company's computer just to pause a video.
💳 Real payments, verified
Subscriptions through the app store's cash register, then OUR server confirms "yes, really subscribed" before unlocking.
WHY Phones can be tricked into claiming a purchase. The server's word is the only one that counts.
📥 Offline downloads with recheck
Save videos for the no-signal gym basement; permission is rechecked at download and at open; canceled subs delete politely.
WHY Offline must work, but "download once, keep forever after canceling" would cheat the trainer out of his income.
🧹 Honesty punch list
Friendly error sentences instead of raw computer errors, real GPS instead of fake, and a brand-new sign-in screen, because the old door had no hallway leading to it.
WHY A QA walkthrough literally could not find a way to log in. Real users would have hit the same wall.
🔌 Real backend turned on
Production database, six server programs, notification hooks, and a seeded demo trainer with real videos, all live-verified.
WHY Code on a laptop is a promise. A running server is a product.
🔍 What the inspectors caught here

When the server genuinely REFUSES a share or a piece of feedback (for example, the coaching link ended), the app still says "Saved offline, will retry!" and later quietly gives up. Being told "saved" about something that can never arrive is an accidental lie, and it is near the top of the fix-it list. Also: the upload progress bar can jump to 100% when it does not know the real total, and a failed video upload can leave behind an orphan thumbnail.

📰 MyNews the newspaper app

Reporters publish, volunteer editors suggest improvements, readers support reporters directly. No ads.

🏗️ The foundation
MyNews became the 41st mini-app in the toolbox, with its own tables and three math engines: text-difference finder, editor trust score, and fair payment splitter.
WHY Every solid house starts with a boring, correct foundation. The engines were built pure and tested first so everything above them can trust them.
🕯️ Wax-seal publishing
Every article is signed with the reporter's secret key. The server checks the seal BEFORE saving. Reader screens, reporter pages, and RSS included.
WHY Nobody, not even the server's owner, should be able to publish fake words under a reporter's name. The math makes forgery impossible instead of just forbidden.
✏️ The editing desk
Editors SUGGEST changes with sources; the author reviews side by side and accepts, rejects, or counter-edits. Only the author can accept. Good edits earn credibility points. Newsrooms share drafts with embargo labels.
WHY Wikipedia proved strangers will fix each other's writing, but news needs the author to stay in charge. Suggestions with author approval give both.
🔍 What the inspectors caught here

Creating a newsroom saves twice in a row (room, then owner). If the second save fails, a ghost newsroom exists that nobody owns or can see. Fix: make both saves happen together or not at all. Logged before the real server gets provisioned.

🖥️ The website, the big merges, and the inspections

The connective tissue: making the web hub a daily driver, landing four teams' work onto one main line, and then hunting our own bugs.

📱 Website acts like an app
Install it on your home screen, notch-safe layout, smarter Today dashboard, NYC event discovery with saving, food-database import, workout CSV exports.
WHY The founder uses it on his phone every day in NYC. A daily-driver test finds real problems faster than any meeting.
🎭 Deleted the fake party
Removed made-up people and made-up likes from the workouts social tab.
WHY An honest empty room beats a fake party. House rule.
🧩 Two merge landings
Carried four teams' work onto the main line twice, each time running the full robot-checker army first. The checkers caught three real bugs: a 40-vs-41 mini-app counter, a settings file made invisible by a hiding rule, and an upload line that shuffled same-millisecond items.
WHY Each team's table looked perfect alone. Bugs live in the cracks BETWEEN tables, and only the full check finds them. We never merge blind.
🕵️ Three layers of inspection
A harsh "could a customer use this today?" ruling on all 8 products (answer: 0 of 8, mostly missing money/legal plumbing). A deep BestChef world-launch inspection. And a code audit where a second, different AI read all four days of code, then every serious claim was re-verified by hand.
WHY Builders fall in love with their own work. Fresh, different eyes are the antidote, and two different brains catch more than one.
📓 The errors notebook
Five new entries went into the errors log, each with the date, exact location, and status: Unresolved until truly fixed.
WHY Hiding mistakes makes software rot. Writing them down turns them into chores that get done.

📖 Little dictionary

Commit: saving your work with a note about what you changed, like a game save slot.
Test: a robot checker that tries the app automatically and yells if something breaks.
Merge: carrying separate teams' work onto one shared main line and making sure it all fits.
Server: a computer that is always on, somewhere else, doing jobs for the app.
Encryption: scrambling a message with a secret code so only the key-holder can read it.
Signature (the math kind): a stamp only you can make but anyone can check. Forging it is practically impossible.
Relay: a post office computer that passes sealed envelopes without being able to open them.
Fails closed: when a safety check breaks, the door stays LOCKED instead of swinging open.
Audit: an inspection where someone hunts for problems in the work on purpose.
Sync: making the copies on two devices agree with each other.
The whole week in one sentence: we took four apps with strong engines and gave them the rest of their bodies, honestly: private messages that never lie about delivery, a cooking contest that speaks 21 languages and resists cheaters, a gym coach that takes real money and voice commands, a newspaper with unforgeable wax seals, and then we invited inspectors to find every flaw and wrote each one in the notebook.
