How it stays private

Sealed envelopes, not postcards.

Every other chat app is a postcard: the company carries it and reads it along the way. Meerkat is a sealed envelope — and when possible, we skip the mail carrier entirely.

Locked on your phone

Everything you send is encrypted on your device with keys that only your community holds. Meerkat the company never has them. We couldn't peek if we wanted to, and we couldn't hand your messages to anyone else either.

Delivered the closest way

Your devices try the direct route first: same Wi-Fi, then phone-to-phone nearby, then the internet. A relay server is the last resort, and even then it only sees a sealed envelope with no name on it.

Honest, always

Meerkat never fakes it. Every "delivered," every "online" dot, every member count is real and verified — or the app tells you plainly it doesn't know. No fake green dots. That rule is enforced in code.

Straight answers

Things you should ask any chat app.

Can Meerkat read my messages?

No — and not as a policy promise, as a math fact. Messages are encrypted on your device with keys only your community's members hold. Our relay servers carry sealed ciphertext addressed by throwaway tokens. There is nothing to read, sell, subpoena, or leak.

Will there ever be ads?

No. Ads require watching you, and we structurally can't watch you. Our whole business is $4.99 for the app and an optional $4.99/mo hosted plan. You are the customer, not the product.

What happens when I lose my phone?

Your other devices hold your communities, and recovery keys let you restore on a new phone. Adding your own new device is a quick pairing — or just tap your phones together.

Is "pay once" too good to last?

The one-time price covers the app. Communities that want us to host their relay and storage pay $4.99/mo — that recurring piece is what keeps the lights on long-term, and it's optional and per-community, not per-person.

Isn't encrypted = sketchy?

Meerkat launches with real safety infrastructure: abuse reporting, content moderation for public spaces, and mandatory child-safety reporting pipelines. Privacy for your community, not impunity for abusers. And the encryption itself follows published open standards, not homemade math.

When can I get it?

Meerkat is finished and in final launch checks — legal review, app-store submission, and on-device verification. We'd rather ship it right than ship it Friday. iOS, Android, and web at launch.
