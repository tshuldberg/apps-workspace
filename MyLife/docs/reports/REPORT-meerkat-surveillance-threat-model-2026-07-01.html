Can Meerkat remove the corporate surveillance web from its users' lives?

SECTION 1Verdict

Yes, with three honest qualifiers. For everything that happens inside Meerkat, the corporate data-harvesting function is removed structurally, not by policy promise: there is no account identity to build a profile on, no plaintext on any server to mine, no server-side social graph to map, no engagement telemetry to model emotions with, and no algorithmic feed or ad system to manipulate. A Meerkat operator cannot sell what the architecture never lets them see.

Qualifier 1: Scope

It only protects what moves into it

Meerkat removes surveillance of communication and community life. The rest of the founder's list (what you buy, eat, when you train) is the MyLife suite's job via local-first modules. The two together attack the whole list; Meerkat alone attacks the social half, which is the most valuable half of the corporate graph.

Qualifier 2: Metadata

Content is dark; the network layer is dim, not dark

A relay operator or ISP still sees IP addresses, ciphertext sizes, and timing. Onion routing is an explicit non-goal in the architecture doc. This is the same residual class Signal accepts. LAN and proximity transports produce zero server records and are Meerkat's structural answer.

Qualifier 3: Deployment

The spine is not deployed yet

DEFAULT_RELAY_URL is ''. No relay, no EAS device build, no two-device QA has run. Every claim in this report describes code that is real and test-covered, but the product cannot take a single user off Facebook until founder-ops turns the spine on.

SECTION 2The surveillance web, decomposed

The founder's framing is accurate and worth stating precisely. Corporate platforms do not primarily sell your messages; they sell the derived model of you, assembled from five collection channels:

ChannelWhat it yieldsCourt-documented consequence
Identity anchorReal name, phone, email tie every action to one durable profileProfiles subpoenaable, breachable, sellable, mergeable across services
Social graphWho you know, who they know, tie strength, contact rhythmCambridge Analytica reached 87M people through 270k consenting users via friend-graph API access
Content inspectionWhat you say, share, and post, scanned for interests and intentInterest and emotion classifiers feed the ad auction
Engagement telemetryWhat you linger on, react to, when you wake, what angers youAnger-weighted ranking amplified incitement in Myanmar (UN and court findings)
Feed controlRanking chooses what masses of people seeEmotional-contagion experiments; election-scale influence operations

Aggregators like Palantir do not need their own app: they fuse platform data, broker purchases, and government records. Their pipeline is only as rich as what upstream collectors hold in plaintext. Starving the collectors starves the fusion layer.

SECTION 4What Meerkat structurally removes, and why "structurally" matters

The difference between Meerkat and a "privacy-respecting" corporate platform is enforcement location. A policy can be changed by an acquisition, a new CEO, a court order, or a quarterly target. Meerkat's guarantees live in places policy cannot reach:

  • The payload, not the channel. Security is sealed into the data itself (signed, encrypted, scope-capped), so it holds across every transport rung including hostile relays. A cm_ table omitted from the sync policy now fails closed to device_local (the C1 audit fix), so a future developer mistake leaks nothing.
  • Honesty as an enforced invariant. The transport-honesty boundary (never fake connectivity, every number from real engine tables, staged is never "sent") is locked by parity guards and tests, not by convention. This is the anti-Theranos property: the marketing cannot drift past the cryptography because the UI is not allowed to claim what the engine did not do.
  • No privileged operator. The relay, hosted nodes, and even Meerkat-the-company are in the threat model. Verify-then-pin means a malicious content host is skipped fail-closed. An owner takedown is an owner-signed protocol message, not an admin database write.
  • Moderation without inspection. evaluatePublishBoundary hash-checks only published_blob content before it leaves the device; private scopes are provably unscanned. Abuse handling exists (mute, block, report, owner review queues, signed descriptor kills) without granting anyone a content-reading capability.
The strategic consequence
If people can genuinely congregate here (communities, posts, DMs, files, calls), then the corporations lose the input, not just the output. There is no feed to bias because ranking is local. There is no graph API to exploit because the graph never exists server-side. There is no emotion model to sell because reactions never leave the device. The surveillance function is not "better protected"; it is unimplementable.

SECTION 6Myanmar, Cambridge Analytica, Palantir: does the architecture prevent a repeat?

HarmWhat it requiredMeerkat equivalent
Myanmar-style algorithmic incitementEngagement-ranked feed rewarding outrage, centralized virality, platform inaction at scaleSTRUCTURALLY BLOCKED No engagement ranking exists to reward outrage; the feed is local and deterministic; trending is verifiable-signals-only. Nobody, including Meerkat, can turn the amplification dial because there is no dial.
Cambridge Analytica-style harvestingGraph API access, like-based psychometrics, ad microtargeting to deliver the manipulationSTRUCTURALLY BLOCKED No graph API, no like telemetry, no ad system to buy delivery through. All three legs of that attack are missing.
Palantir-style fusion surveillanceUpstream plaintext: platform data, broker feeds, compelled recordsSTARVED, NOT BLOCKED For Meerkat-resident activity there is no plaintext to acquire and no who-knows-whom record to compel. A compelled relay could log IPs and timing; proximity/LAN transports do not even produce that. The fusion layer loses its richest feed but keeps non-Meerkat sources.
The honest counterweight: encrypted networks have their own failure mode
Removing the platform's eyes also removes platform-level moderation. WhatsApp's lynching-rumor crisis in India happened inside E2E encryption, driven by frictionless forwarding, not by an algorithm. Meerkat's current posture is genuinely better positioned than WhatsApp was (no forward-count mechanics, no virality machinery, human-scale communities, local safety tools, owner review queues, signed takedowns on the public layer, a publish-boundary hash check, and planned humanity verification), but two real gaps remain before this claim is defensible at scale: production member removal with epoch rotation (Plan 28, engine exists with zero callers) and the deployed abuse-scanning service for the public archive (founder-ops Tier D). Combating Facebook credibly means being able to answer "so how do YOU handle Myanmar?" and the answer must be these mechanisms, shipped.

SECTION 8What could be improved (ranked)

TIER 0 Turn the spine on. Nothing else matters until this.
  • Deploy the first-party zero-knowledge relay, verify /healthz over TLS externally, then flip DEFAULT_RELAY_URL. Out of the box the app currently has no connectivity at all.
  • Run pnpm install for the declared native deps, set the EAS projectId, cut a dev/EAS build, and execute the two-device exit demo in apps/meerkat/Tickets/device-qa-exit-demo.md. Every physical transport rung is unverified on hardware.
  • Restore CI (GitHub billing). Local-green is currently the only trust gate for a product whose whole pitch is trustworthiness.
TIER 1 Close the daily-driver survival gaps

A person cannot leave WhatsApp or Discord for an app that has no DM button and only syncs when both people open a screen at the same time.

  • Finish DMs end to end (Plan 21 Phases 2-10): the protocol is shipped; the UI is behind DM_MESSAGES_SURFACE_AVAILABLE = false.
  • Seamless auto-connect and background mesh (Plan 29): manual sessions are the single biggest usability gap versus every incumbent. This is where privacy products historically lose the mainstream.
  • Real member removal + epoch key rotation (Plan 28): commitMemberRemoval exists with zero production callers, and the legacy revoke path is fake. Both a security and a community-safety launch requirement.
  • Seal the rendezvous identity bundle (Plan 23 D.5): the one known metadata leak that exceeds the sizes-and-timing baseline.
  • Finish FF3 owner-side public join so joining a public community is one tap, not a support conversation.
TIER 2 Metadata hardening (go beyond Signal's residual)
  • Padding and cover traffic on relay sessions to blunt size-and-timing analysis, the strongest remaining capability of a hostile relay.
  • Multi-relay diversity and rotation so no single operator ever observes a user's full session history; the ranked-choice transport design already gives this a natural home.
  • Optional proxy/Tor dialing for IP privacy in high-risk contexts (journalists, activists: the users who most need the pitch to be true).
  • Push privacy option: a polling-only mode for users who do not want APNs/FCM learning message timing.
  • MLS/TreeKEM migration path for group keys, so the "not MLS" caveat eventually retires and communities scale cryptographically.
TIER 3 Adoption weapons (the actual hard problem)

Facebook's power is the network effect, not the technology. Meerkat's crypto is ahead of its UX: the 2026-07-01 benchmark eval scored the UI roughly 4/10 against the broad-audience bar.

  • Execute the UI benchmark roadmap (suggested Plan 30): join friction, real-time feel, and information architecture versus Discord/Signal/TikTok expectations.
  • Open public participation + humanity verification together (Plans 26 + 24): public posting is the Instagram/Reddit/X replacement surface, and it is explicitly not shippable without the anti-bot gate.
  • Calls and rooms (Plan 25): table stakes for the Discord half of the wedge.
  • The Slack/Workplace import wedge from the Posts spec: erosion via interop beats migration-by-persuasion, and the Workplace shutdown (hard delete 2026-06-01) is a live migration moment.
  • Proximity communities (Plan 27): in-person-gated groups are a genuinely differentiated feature no incumbent can copy without abandoning their data model.
  • Publish the protocol: the agent-native open-standard direction in the Posts spec is the long-term moat against "just another walled garden, but smaller." A protocol others can implement makes the anti-surveillance claim bigger than one company's promise.
TIER 4 Governance and sustainability (keep the claim true over time)
  • Deploy the real abuse-scanning service for the public archive (the code hook exists; the scanner is founder-ops and a hard store-review gate).
  • Prove the economics: $4.99 one-time plus freemium hosting must sustainably pay for relays and community nodes at scale, or the pressure to monetize data returns through the back door. Publish the cost model.
  • External security audit + reproducible builds: the honesty boundary is enforced internally by tests; third-party verification is what makes it credible to the people Meerkat is for.
  • Write the "how we handle Myanmar" document: a public moderation-capabilities statement (what owners can do, what Meerkat can and cannot see, what the descriptor-kill lever is and who audits it) before critics write it for you.
  • Legal readiness: the founder-ops bundle (privacy policy, data-deletion page, encryption export self-classification, store UGC policy review) is part of the anti-surveillance story, not paperwork.

SECTION 9Bottom line

Meerkat's architecture genuinely does what the mission claims: within its walls, the corporate surveillance function is not restricted, it is absent. No identity anchor, no server-side graph, no plaintext, no engagement model, no manipulable feed, no ad machine, and a business model that never needs any of them. That combination is rare; most "private" platforms keep at least the graph and the telemetry.

The gap between architecture and impact is now almost entirely non-cryptographic: an undeployed relay spine, a DM surface behind a flag, manual-only sessions, an unshipped removal/rotation path, and a UI that is not yet good enough to pull ordinary people away from the incumbents. The corporations' moat is habit and network effect, and that moat is attacked with Tier 0 and Tier 1 above, not with more cryptography.

Answer to the founder's question: yes, Meerkat can take this function away from the corporations for its users, and unlike a policy-based competitor it can prove it. But "for its users" is the operative phrase. The path from here is: turn the spine on, finish DMs and auto-connect, ship removal and humanity verification, then win the UX fight. The privacy is done being invented; it now has to be deployed, and then it has to be wanted.
