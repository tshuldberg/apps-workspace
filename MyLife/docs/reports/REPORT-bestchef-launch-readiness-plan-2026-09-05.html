Verdict

The product is real. The launch is not yet a global launch.

BestChef has a strong, honest engineering core: comprehensive row-level security, durable server-side rate limits, a fail-closed moderation model, a real 24-locale i18n engine, and every fabricated output from the July audit removed. All suites and gates ran green in this session. What stands between the code and a worldwide launch is no longer bugs. It is scope that was never built (Android, a public web presence, cross-language reading, moderation coverage in 24 languages), operations that were never stood up (vendors, legal entity, live database migrations, builds), and content that does not exist yet (20 seed recipes for a platform whose home screen promises a Top 100).

Understanding the app

One idea, three loops, two halves

The idea. For any dish there is a best recipe, and a community that has actually cooked it can find that recipe together. Every other recipe product ranks by popularity or editorial taste; BestChef ranks by reviewed votes, where a vote that carries a CookProof photo of the plated dish counts three times a swipe vote. That is the wedge against Cookpad (community volume, regional silos), Allrecipes and Tasty (editorial catalogs), and Paprika or Samsung Food (private organizers).

Loop 1: Compete

A chef picks a dish from the catalog, submits a recipe with plated photos, ingredients, steps and an optional 5-minute cook-along video, and enters that dish's Top 100. Submissions land in a moderation queue before anyone sees them.

Loop 2: Judge

Everyone else swipes through a full-screen vote deck. A double-tap likes. Tapping the seal opens a Reviewed Vote: four tiers (Not for me, I'd eat that, Momma's, Best Chef), notes, and a mandatory CookProof photo that is itself screened before it counts.

Loop 3: Cook

A private kitchen that never leaves the device: saved recipes, grocery lists built from ingredients, a pantry with expirations, receipt and grocery-photo import with human confirmation, barcode lookup, and a hands-free cook mode.

The two halves

Cloud social layer on Supabase (auth, Postgres with RLS, private storage, 12 edge functions, pg_cron jobs). Private kitchen on local SQLite through the shared @mylife/bestchef module (schema v34). Public flows never ride the mesh sync layer, by design.

Fresh perspective

Findings the July work did not cover

Ranked by how much they change the launch. None of these are code defects in what exists; they are gaps between the current scope and a complete global launch with every feature live on day one.

N1 Scope

"Global launch" is currently iOS-only

app.json declares platforms: ["ios"], there is no Android native project and no Android EAS profile. Android holds roughly 70 percent of smartphones worldwide and well over 90 percent in India, Indonesia, Vietnam and Brazil, which are exactly the markets the Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Indonesian, Vietnamese and Brazilian Portuguese catalogs target. Launching 24 languages on iOS alone ships the translations to a minority of the people they were written for.

Plan: WS-A builds the Android app from the same Expo codebase (prebuild, FCM push, per-script fonts, RTL, camera, Sentry, Play Console with data safety, IARC content rating and UGC policy), with device QA in every launch locale. This is the single largest engineering item in the plan and it is a founder decision to include it; the plan treats it as required because the ask is a global launch with nothing deferred.
N2 Scope

There is no BestChef web presence, and the app already depends on one

Every share action sends https://bestchef.app/recipe/<id>. The onboarding gate links Terms and Community Guidelines at bestchef.app. Settings links Privacy, Data Deletion and Support there. App Review checks that the privacy URL resolves. Today the domain serves nothing, so the first share a user sends is a dead link and the first legal tap is a 404. The hub web recipes adapter is not a public BestChef site and must not be presented as one.

Plan: WS-B ships apps/bestchef-web: legal pages, support, DSA point of contact, a landing page, and public read-only dish, recipe and chef pages (approved media via signed URLs, localized to all 24 languages) with Universal Links and Android App Links so a tap opens the app when installed. Mocked in the New Surfaces section.
N3 Global

Recipes cannot be read across languages

The onboarding copy says it plainly: "Recipe content stays native. Interface copy uses your selected language." A Thai chef's Pad Thai is unreadable to the Spanish, Hindi or Arabic cook who wants to vote on it. The vote deck defaults to "My language", which quietly turns a global leaderboard into 24 small ones. Cookpad solves this with regional silos; BestChef's thesis (one best recipe per dish, worldwide) needs the opposite.

Plan: WS-D adds server-side machine translation of submission title, ingredients and steps on demand, cached in bc_submission_translations, provider behind the existing CHF-1 quota seam, rendered with a "Translated from Thai, see original" label. Cross-language deck and leaderboard become the default; "My language" stays as a filter.
N4 Safety

Moderation is fail-closed in 24 languages with an English-only console and no moderators

Fail-closed is correct. It also means that on launch day every submission, comment and CookProof in 24 languages waits for a human who reads it. The console shows content verbatim, has no translation assist, no language routing and no SLA view. Without vendors and staffing, the queue becomes the product's bottleneck within hours of a successful launch.

Plan: WS-C contracts image and text classification (NSFW, violence, food-vs-not-food, hate and harassment text in all 24 languages), a child-safety hash-match provider, registers with NCMEC, adds console machine translation and language routing, and staffs a moderator rota with a written SLA (image review under 4 hours, reports under 24 hours, appeals under 7 days).
N5 Store

The age-rating plan references a tier Apple removed

The July runbook says "complete the age-rating questionnaire (12+)". Apple replaced 12+ and 17+ with 13+, 16+ and 18+ in 2025 and made the expanded questionnaire mandatory before any submission after 2026-01-31. A user-generated-content app with a 13-to-16 age gate should expect 13+ and must answer the new questions on in-app controls, capabilities and UGC. On Android the equivalent is the IARC questionnaire in Play Console.

Plan: WS-E answers both questionnaires from the actual feature set, aligns the in-app gates, and re-checks the App Store metadata pipeline's age-rating fields.
N6 Legal

Storefront-specific safety law is not in the plan

The legal work so far covers GDPR export, DSA statements of reasons and per-country age gates. A 175-storefront launch also triggers: the UK Online Safety Act (illegal-content risk assessment and children's access assessment are live duties for user-to-user services since March and April 2025), the EU DSA's designated point of contact and annual transparency report for hosting services, and India's IT Rules grievance officer requirement for intermediaries if the India storefront opens. None of these need engineering beyond a contact page and a reports export, but they need documents and named people before launch.

Plan: WS-E produces the two Ofcom assessments, the DSA contact and transparency report template, the India grievance contact, and folds them into the web legal pages. Counsel review is founder-owned.
N7 Content

The home screen promises a Top 100 and the database has 20 recipes

The seed script loads 20 ChefTom editorial recipes (titles inspired by a Food Network list). Demo fixtures are correctly gated off in public builds, so a launch-day user sees "All caught up" on Vote, "No entries yet" on Top 100 and "Be the first chef to submit" on every dish. Cold start is the number one reason community platforms die in week one, and the 24-locale launch multiplies it: an Indonesian cook needs Indonesian dishes with Indonesian recipes on day one.

Plan: WS-F seeds a localized dish catalog per launch cuisine, recruits 5 to 10 founding chefs per language (the Reddit tiering docs already exist under docs/marketing), and ships one editorial recipe per top-20 dish per cuisine under an approved seed-content provenance, honestly labeled.
N8 Scale

Video still streams raw MP4 from origin

Audit H7 stayed open "by design" for early waves. A global, all-storefront launch is not an early wave. Up to 150 MB per upload, played from Supabase Storage with no transcoding, no adaptive bitrate and no CDN, will be slow on Indian and Brazilian mobile networks and expensive on egress.

Plan: WS-G integrates Mux or Cloudflare Stream behind the existing media-finalize path (upload to provider, store playback ID, signed playback, thumbnails), keeps the re-sign job for legacy assets, and sets a per-account monthly upload cap.
N9 Store

iPad is declared supported but never designed or tested

supportsTablet: true means App Review runs the app on iPad and the listing needs iPad screenshots. No layout in the app adapts beyond phone width; the review screenshots are phone-only.

Plan: WS-H either ships responsive layouts for the five tabs and detail screens on iPad and captures iPad screenshots, or sets supportsTablet: false before submission. Recommendation: ship iPad, because the kitchen (cook mode, grocery) is a natural tablet use.
N10 Release

No build contains the remediation, and no device evidence exists for it

TestFlight build 24 predates the barcode scanner, push entitlement, expo-updates, Sentry and every plan 45 change. There is no end-to-end device test automation, no per-locale screenshot run, and no device proof that Arabic and Hebrew RTL, Indic font chains or CJK typography render correctly on hardware.

Plan: WS-H cuts iOS and Android builds, adds Maestro flows for the seven core journeys, runs a device matrix in all 24 locales (which doubles as the localized store-screenshot source), and records the results as launch evidence.
N11 Config

Three locales are half-registered

Bengali, Tamil and Telugu have full catalogs, fonts and push copy, but app.json lists 21 iOS supportedLocales and the store-metadata directory has 21 files. iOS will not advertise those languages and the App Store listing cannot show them.

Plan: WS-D adds the three codes to both lists and generates their store metadata; a parity check between catalogs, supportedLocales and store metadata joins check:parity.
N12 Ops

Production has never run the current schema

Eleven migrations from 07-11 have not been applied to staging or production. bc_job_config rows are absent (scheduled jobs silently no-op without them). No PITR, backup or restore-drill evidence exists for the production project, and the alert scheduler is a sample workflow, not a running one.

Plan: WS-I applies and verifies migrations on staging then production, seeds job config with --verify, upgrades to Supabase Pro with PITR, performs and records a restore drill, wires alerting, and publishes a status page.
N13 Risk

Cadence is the meta-risk

Every burst delivered; every gap stalled. Plan 33 estimated 12 to 16 weeks of solo-founder-plus-agents time. Nine weeks have passed since plan 45 with no execution. The vendor and legal items are calendar-bound and only start when the founder starts them.

Plan: Week 1 of the timeline is founder-only: sign vendor contracts, engage counsel, apply migrations. Everything agents can build proceeds in parallel and does not wait on those, but launch cannot happen without them.
