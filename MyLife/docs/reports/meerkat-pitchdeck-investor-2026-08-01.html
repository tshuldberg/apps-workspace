The problem

Human communities live on rented land.

Every group of people online today sits on one of three business models — and each one has now visibly failed its users.

1 · Rent, per head, forever

Slack charges $7.25–$15 per user per month.1 A 25-person community on Slack Pro pays $2,175 every year — and in May 2025 Salesforce banned exporting, archiving, or indexing your own message history while its own AI kept reading it.2

2 · "Free," paid in surveillance

Meta extracted ~$233/year of ad revenue per North American user in 2024 (~$57 worldwide).3 Discord stores every text message in server-readable plaintext — and in October 2025 confirmed ~70,000 users' government ID photos leaked through a support vendor.4

3 · Platform mortality

Facebook Workplace had 7 million paying users. Meta shut it down anyway to chase AI, and on June 1, 2026 — eight weeks ago — permanently deleted all remaining data.5 Every hosted community is one strategy memo away from a deletion date.

Why now

Twenty-four months that broke trust in every incumbent.

US officials leaked strike plans into a Signal group with a journalist present. Signal downloads jumped ~30% globally, +45% in the US, within 48 hours. Privacy demand is event-driven and explosive.

No bulk export, no archives, no indexes, no LLM use of your own messages — while Slack's own AI retains full access. Your history, their asset.

Ads in Status and Channels after a decade of "never." By December 2025, AI-chat data feeds ad targeting with no blanket US opt-out. Encrypted content, monetized context.

Oct 2025

Age-verification ID photos, spilled through one third-party support vendor, followed by extortion. Centralized identity collection is a honeypot.

Jan 2026

Confidential S-1 filed. Public-market pressure means ads and commerce on the platform hosting 28M communities for free, with no text E2EE.

Jul 2026

Suspicionless scanning of unencrypted messages is legal in the EU until 2028; end-to-end encrypted platforms cleared an exclusion. Regulation is now actively sorting the market into readable and unreadable. Meerkat is built on the right side of that line.

Product

Discord's shape. Signal's guarantees.
Your hardware, if you want.

Meerkat gives every community its own encrypted private server — without anyone needing to know what a server is. Three golden flows: add my device · share with a friend · start a community.

Full community surface

Communities, channels, threads-as-posts, DMs, friends, voice/video calls and rooms (LiveKit), file library with reader and player, public discover tier, and an OS share sheet. 31 mobile routes, 21 web areas, in parity.

A mesh, not a mothership

Deterministic transport ladder: LAN Wi-Fi → nearby peer (Multipeer / Wi-Fi Direct) → BLE wake → WebRTC → encrypted relay. Devices talk directly when they can; relays only ever forward ciphertext addressed by ephemeral tokens. Two phones on the same Wi-Fi need no internet at all.

Store-and-forward, like Signal

Send a message days after your friend was last online and it arrives — a mailbox layer parks sealed envelopes and drains them on any transport. Offline is a normal state, not an error.

Posts: the Workplace successor

The core primitive is the post — top-level comments, nested branches, and activity that bumps a post back to the top. The context-management model 7M people paid Meta for, rebuilt on encrypted rails, with agents designed in as first-class participants.

In-person trust ceremonies

Add a friend by tapping phones: a mutual confirm with a short authentication code over local radio, zero servers involved. Identity linking collapses a person's devices into one verified person across the app.

Bring your own storage

Eight storage destinations shipped: iCloud, Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Box, S3, WebDAV, or a connected server — encrypted before it leaves the device. Or pay Meerkat $4.99/mo and never think about it.

Technology & moat

Standards-grade cryptography, and a codebase that refuses to lie.

The crypto is real

Noise NK handshakes with full transcript binding (X25519 · XSalsa20-Poly1305 · SHA-512). Anonymous credentials via RFC 9474 RSA blind signatures in the Privacy Pass token shape — the billing server can verify you paid without ever being able to link you to what you do. Ed25519-signed events everywhere, HKDF domain separation, epoch key rotation on member removal, and a dedicated red-team test suite on the handshake layer.

The honesty boundary is the brand

A founder-enforced rule: never fake connectivity. Every peer count, delivery state, and presence dot is derived from verified signed rows or says "unavailable" in plain language. A hostile launch audit independently called this out as "unusually strong." In a market that just watched Slack, Meta, and Discord get caught saying one thing and doing another, verifiable honesty is the moat.

Scope is enforced in the data model

Every synced entity declares a scope — device-local → personal → shared workspace → published — with hard caps so private data can never escalate outward. Security lives in the payload, not the channel.

A $20–40B rent market, an exploding creator economy — and an almost empty column where Meerkat sits.

The empty column

Consumers spent $155.8B in app stores in 2025 (+21.6% YoY) — but direct consumer spend on encrypted messaging apps is ~$357M/yr, ~0.2% of that.7 Not because demand is absent: because the two category leaders are a donation-funded nonprofit and a freemium app that most users wrongly believe is encrypted. Nobody has seriously tried to sell private community infrastructure at a consumer price. That column is the opportunity.

Demand is measured, not asserted

81% of Americans are concerned how companies use their data and 81% expect it to be misused (Pew, 2023). 33% of global consumers are "Privacy Actives" who have already switched providers over data practices — 49% among 25–34-year-olds (Cisco, 2023–24). 36% have deleted an app over privacy (Usercentrics, 2025).8

Moderation on encrypted rails — safety tooling (malware scan, abuse reporting, NCMEC filing queue, DMCA intake, moderation console) shipped before launch, not after the first crisis.
