Executive Summary

MyNews is worth building, and the founder has locked its shape: a standalone app (apps/mynews) paired with a hub module (modules/mynews, table prefix nw_), monetized by a $4.99 one-time unlock on the same Manhattan pattern the suite already runs. That shape survives scrutiny, not because the price is dropped, but because the things that historically kill decentralized news apps are designed out: reading stays free, the substrate is reused rather than rebuilt, the trust spine is individual journalists, and there is no token.

Verdict The problem MyNews attacks is painkiller-grade and population-scale. The willingness to pay for news is the soft axis, so MyNews answers it by gating the right thing. Reading is free in both surfaces; the $4.99 unlocks the power layer (the saveable algorithm-web of named feeds, advanced filters, the full open-science section, offline, and the Blindspot tools). Tips flow to journalists off-app, MyLife Pro bundles the unlock for suite users, and a 501(c)(3) carries the open-science half. The one-time fee is honored as an impulse-priced growth signal, not as the engine that funds a perpetual cost curve.

The grievance is population-scale, not niche

The demand signal on the problem axis is strong and durable. US adult trust in mass media sits near 28 percent in Gallup's 2025 reading, the lowest in roughly fifty years of polling. News avoidance is rising and is driven mainly by negativity: the Reuters Institute 2024 Digital News Report found 39 percent sometimes or often avoid news, up from 29 percent in 2017, and valued perspective and being inspired above being updated. The appetite for algorithm control is proven by adoption, not just surveys: Bluesky reached about 40 million users with 50,000-plus custom feeds and no token, and Substack passed 5 million paid subscriptions on roughly $450M of writer revenue, showing people pay for trusted humans and composable tooling, not for platforms or tokens.

Why $4.99 is safe here: free read, paid power

The willingness-to-pay caution in the prior research is real (about 18 percent pay for online news in wealthy markets, and 71 percent of non-payers say no offer would convert them), and the founder has chosen $4.99 anyway. That choice is honored and de-risked, not argued against. Reading is ungated on both the standalone app and the hub module, so reach, the journalist-trust flywheel, community notes, and the public archive are never paywalled (this also keeps the app from being classified as primarily paid news, lowering store-rejection risk). The $4.99 mylife_mynews_unlock entitlement lights up the power layer only: building and saving the algorithm-web of named, shareable feeds, advanced filters, the full open-science section, offline reading, and the Blindspot tools. Tips are always on for free and paid users alike, web-routed via Stripe Connect so journalists keep about 97 percent and the IAP cut is avoided. MyLife Pro auto-grants the unlock, so suite users never pay twice. The honest unit-economics note stays: a one-time $4.99 nets about $3.49 at the 30 percent standard rate (about $4.24 under the Small Business Program), and a single front-loaded payment cannot fund continuous serving, relay and seed hosting, moderation labor, and DOI fees. The resolution is the rail mix, not the price: the recurring MyLife Pro subscription is the annuity that carries the cost curve and converts the free-read audience over time, and the open-science half is funded by a 501(c)(3) and grants (Press Forward alone is deploying $500M-plus) that app revenue could never match.

Build on Meerkat, do not rebuild

MyNews is a branded surface over Meerkat's existing substrate, not a second SQLite module that re-implements signing, content-addressing, federation, and moderation. Extend the signed post type to article and preprint (signature-covered metadata, so no new signing code), add one open-science metadata sidecar (nw_article_meta: DOI, ORCID authors, license, rights route, embargo), and reach public scope only through Plan 19's signed publish path to cp_publications. The architecture is a deliberate hybrid: a server-backed canonical record (durable archive, DOI registration via Crossref or DataCite, and harm moderation), because IPFS and mesh replication do not guarantee permanence and cannot anchor a citable record, layered with portable signed identity and an AT-Protocol-style composable-feed and composable-labeler stack. This matches MyLife's BestChef public-launch exception and transport-honesty rule.

Trust is people, not outlets

The brand promise is that you trust individual journalists by what they have published and gotten right, never the masthead they sit under. Two first-class tiers carry it: a VERIFIED tier (claimable bylines, an earned and optional badge, identity or affiliation proof via domain email, ORCID, or manual review, plus a per-journalist track record of corrections and accuracy) and an OPEN PSEUDONYMOUS tier (a persistent handle backed by key continuity, with trust earned purely through track record and community notes). Bias is rendered Ground-News-style and built to beat it: source and author ratings on two axes (lean and factuality), coverage-from-all-sides, and a Blindspot surfacer, always citing which org produced each number and showing their disagreement rather than asserting one MyNews verdict. The founder's original whole-page-color idea was reshaped into discrete, multi-encoded badges and a spectrum bar, for accessibility, internationalization, and neutrality reasons detailed in the next section.

Insight The moat is not any single algorithm, which is commoditized. It is the combination: a portable per-journalist reputation, transparent user-controlled feed composition, a news-plus-open-science domain, privacy-first defaults, and suite distribution, all riding the shared Meerkat substrate. That bundle is hard to copy and moves with regulation (EU DSA Article 27 already mandates recommender transparency and a non-profiling feed option), so MyNews builds compliance by construction instead of fighting the grain.

The Idea, Restated

MyNews fuses two ambitions into one product that ships as a standalone app plus a paired hub module. The first is a decentralized news reader where you own your identity, follow journalists you trust, compose and save your own feeds, and choose who labels the truth, on a record no single media owner controls. The second is an open-science layer where research is published, reviewed, and curated in the open with verifiable authorship. This section restates the founder's now-locked concept in concrete, buildable mechanics and names the closest system that already ships each piece.

Status The direction is locked (2026-06-29). MyNews is a standalone Expo app (apps/mynews) and a paired hub module (modules/mynews, prefix nw_), exactly like Meerkat, BestChef, DoWork, and Manhattan. Both surfaces import the same business logic and render the same signed Meerkat substrate; the only difference is chrome and entry point. Standalone is a packaging decision, not a pricing one: reading is free in both, and the $4.99 unlock is an entitlement that lights up either surface. None of the building blocks below are science fiction; what is missing is a single product that fuses them and points them at news plus open science.

Pillar 0: the trust spine is individual journalists

Before any feed mechanic, the unit of trust is a person, not an outlet. Two tiers are both first-class. The VERIFIED tier carries claimable bylines, an earned and optional badge, optional identity or affiliation proof (domain email such as a verified nytimes.com address, ORCID for science authorship, or manual review for at-risk reporters who cannot expose a domain email), a per-journalist track record of corrections and accuracy, and follow-a-journalist feeds. The OPEN PSEUDONYMOUS tier carries a persistent handle backed by key continuity, with no identity disclosure required and trust earned purely through track record and community notes. The crucial framing shown in the UI: verification proves where someone works or that a byline is genuinely theirs, not that they are right, so the verified badge is kept visually and semantically separate from accuracy reputation. A long-running pseudonymous reporter with a correction-honest record can out-rank a freshly verified one. Reputation is portable because it is keyed to the journalist's Ed25519 identity, so a media owner cannot strip it.

Pillar 1: a saveable algorithm-web of named feeds (the power layer)

The founder pictures a visual web of algorithm controls a user saves as multiple named feeds: positive-news, bad-news, event-cluster, and leaning-filtered feeds. The core mechanic already ships. Bluesky's AT Protocol treats a feed generator as an external service that returns only a skeleton (an ordered list of post IDs the app hydrates), users subscribe to feeds the way they follow accounts, and most pin three to five and swipe between them. The visual web exists in pieces too: SkyFeed's no-code block editor, Gobo.social's user-facing sliders, and Bluesky's Attie, which builds feeds from natural-language commands. MyNews emits one nw_feed_def graph from two on-ramps: a visual block canvas and a describe-the-feed-you-want prompt. This is the heart of the $4.99 power layer: free users get a non-profiling default feed plus follow-a-journalist, and building and saving the algorithm-web of named feeds is the paid unlock.

Pillar 2: the advanced filters inside each feed

The filters are a constructive/sentiment control, event clustering, topic and entity filters, recency and velocity, source leaning, and an add-perspective block that injects under-covered outlets. Event clustering is the hardest pillar but solved enough: a production pipeline deduplicates with locality-sensitive hashing (about 80 percent of articles are near-duplicates), then density-clusters the rest on embeddings plus named-entity features. Most stories are single-article, so an event-cluster feed only adds value past a size or velocity threshold. Source-level leaning feeds are realistic; per-article left/center/right labels are noisy and must be marked estimated. The demand for the constructive feed is validated (39 percent avoid news, mostly for mood), but a frictionless mood filter is itself a systematized form of the avoidance the product claims to solve, so every subtractive control is paired with a show-what-was-filtered transparency layer and an instrumented broaden guardrail. These advanced filters sit behind the power unlock alongside the algorithm-web.

Pillar 3: bias labeling, reshaped from page-coloring

The founder wanted the interface to signal bias by coloring the page by political lean. This is where the idea was most cautionary, and it was reshaped, not dropped. The model to emulate and beat is Ground News, which aggregates 40,000-plus outlets but does not run its own ratings: it averages AllSides, Ad Fontes, and MBFC. Four of the five major systems rate the source as a whole and say a source-level rating does not measure the bias of any specific article. Whole-page tint fails on three counts worth recording: accessibility (color-only signaling fails WCAG 1.4.1 Level A and the roughly 1 in 12 men with color-vision deficiency); internationalization (the red/blue mapping is a post-2000 US convention inverted across most of the world, which breaks for a global audience); and correctness (rater divergence concentrates in exactly the center-rated outlets a tinted page would most often mislabel). The replacement keeps the same intent with safe execution: a neutral surface, bias as a discrete multi-encoded badge (labeled spectrum bar plus text plus shape, never color alone), a this-rates-the-outlet-not-this-article disclaimer, two axes that separate reliability from lean, a coverage-from-all-sides distribution, and a Blindspot surfacer that shows what your usual feed is under-covering.

Pillar 4: community-notes moderation, reusing Meerkat hooks

The founder wants crowd moderation at near-zero staff cost. The reference is bridging-based ranking, which surfaces a note only when raters who normally disagree both rate it helpful, so a one-sided brigade cannot push a note over by raw numbers. It is real and open-source, but it cannot be the launch trust layer: only about 5 to 9 percent of notes are ever shown, the median is 10 to 26 hours to display, and a coordinated 5 to 20 percent of raters can still shift outcomes. So MyNews reuses Meerkat's existing layered moderation hooks rather than building new ones: a swappable advisory ML triage classifier, one opinionated default Ozone-style labeler plus composable third-party labelers, and bridging-ranked community notes reserved for durable claims, feeding both the per-article notes and the Blindspot surfacer. The launch-blocking legal floor (UGC four, DMCA designated agent, 48-hour NCII pipeline, DSA Article 16/17 and EU contact) is built once into the shared shell.

Pillar 5: the open-science half

The second ambition lets researchers publish freely, run open peer review, and verify who is qualified to review. The model that maps directly is Publish-Review-Curate, where every reviewed submission becomes a public reviewed preprint. Credential verification has a standards-based answer to adopt rather than invent: ORCID with verified-affiliation Trust Markers plus institutional SSO, layered with a PREreview-style track-record ladder (Trust Markers prove affiliation, not subject expertise). The legal line is load-bearing: MyNews can host preprints, accepted manuscripts under the Rights Retention Strategy, and native CC BY articles, but hosting the publisher's version of record is the Sci-Hub line, so the module verifies rights per upload. The Rights Retention Strategy faces publisher equivocation and varies by jurisdiction. The full open-science section sits behind the power unlock, and the arm is run by a 501(c)(3) for diamond open access, commission-free in-app donations, and grant capital.

Pillar 6: monetization and decentralization, restated

The founder's monetization is a $4.99 one-time purchase plus tips, paying journalists, on a decentralized network no single media owner controls. Restated against the evidence and honored as locked: keep the $4.99 as a non-consumable mylife_mynews_unlock IAP (the Manhattan pattern), but unlock only the power layer so reading stays free; keep tips web-routed, because Apple is forcing content-connected payments through IAP at 30 percent and per-post tips are content-connected; bundle the unlock free for MyLife Pro; and ship no token. Journalists are paid via off-app Stripe Connect (keeping about 97 percent) plus an optional capped, published creator pool distributed by engagement (the Medium model carries no second store cut). Decentralization is delivered as a trust and portability feature (signed identity, content-addressed durable hosting, an export-and-re-host story) layered on a server-backed, subscription-funded core, never as the business model itself.

Market Landscape and Competitors

The decentralized and independent news space splits into four cohorts, and the most useful lessons sit at the boundaries: where one model is a graveyard, the adjacent model is the proven money. The locked direction does not ask MyNews to invent any of these rails. It reuses the Meerkat / @mylife/sync substrate (signed posts, the Plan 19 public/forever archive, mesh sync, moderation hooks, the hosted relay) under a branded standalone surface, ships as both apps/mynews and a paired modules/mynews hub module (prefix nw_), and monetizes with a separate $4.99 one-time unlock on the Manhattan pattern plus tips plus MyLife Pro inclusion.

Cohort 1: The token graveyard

Civil is the canonical cautionary tale. The ConsenSys-backed blockchain-journalism project raised only about $1.4M against an $8M token target in October 2018, refunded every buyer, and forced readers through a 44-step token-purchase flow gated behind a crypto-knowledge quiz (Nieman Lab). It shut in June 2020 when ConsenSys cut funding and the team was folded into decentralized-ID work; foundation president Vivian Schiller's epitaph was that it was "overcomplicated and hard to explain" (Poynter, CoinDesk). Steemit is a decline rather than a death: the STEEM crash drove roughly 70% staff layoffs in 2018, Justin Sun's Tron acquired it in 2020, the community forked Hive, and engagement decayed into bot noise (Cointelegraph, Wikipedia). Minds claims 14M-plus users and a token, but that figure is self-reported (the last independently cited number is roughly 2.5M registered and 300K monthly actives in May 2020), and it became a free-speech destination, not a journalism one (Wikipedia).

Warning The failure mode is the token itself, not on-chain payments. Nostr's value-for-value "zaps" are Bitcoin Lightning micropayments (an existing off-chain rail, not a bespoke token), and the network passed 5 million zaps in May 2025 (OnNostr, nostr.how). MyNews keeps the same discipline: reader-to-journalist tips ride web-routed Stripe / Stripe Connect (journalist keeps ~97%), the $4.99 unlock is a non-consumable IAP, and there is no token to onboard, regulate, or explain.

Cohort 2: The proven money

Direct creator subscriptions are the only model in this space with priced rounds and durable revenue. Substack hit a $1.1B valuation on a $100M Series C (July 2025), passed 5M-plus paid subscriptions, and pays roughly $450M gross writer revenue on a flat 10% take (Tubefilter). Ghost is a Singapore nonprofit with no investors, a 0% take, past $8.5M revenue and $100M-plus in publisher earnings, and shipped ActivityPub federation in v6. Medium had its first profitable month in August 2024 after burning $2.6M/month, with 1M-plus paid members, but only after a 2023 payout overhaul cut many writers' earnings 70 to 90%. Tangle, Isaac Saul's non-partisan politics newsletter, took no investors and reached $4.15M revenue in 2025 from roughly 325,000 subscribers, 85% from subscriptions.

Two lessons carry directly into the locked design. First, the unit of trust is the individual voice, which is exactly why MyNews makes verified-plus-pseudonymous individual journalists (not outlets) the trust spine, with portable reputation keyed to an Ed25519 identity. Second, high earners defect toward flat-fee tools the moment a percentage take stops being capped, so the optional MyNews creator pool is capped and its take rate is published (favoring Ko-fi's low-and-transparent bar over Substack's uncapped 10%), and tips flow off-app so journalists keep ~97%.

Cohort 3: The bias-comparison wedge

This is the closest analog, and the locked direction names Ground News as the primary comparable to emulate and beat. Ground News (Kitchener, CEO Harleen Kaur) aggregates 40,000-plus outlets, generates roughly 30,000 AI summaries a day, paywalls its factuality ratings, briefly reached the #1 free news app slot, and drew about 8M monthly web visits (CJR, Similarweb). Critically, Ground News does not run its own bias methodology: it averages AllSides, Media Bias/Fact Check, and Ad Fontes, and AllSides and MBFC told CJR there is no formal agreement or compensation (Wikipedia). AllSides itself runs a Bloomberg-style B2B model (more than 72% client services in 2022). Particle.news (ex-Twitter's Sara Beykpour, roughly $15.3M raised) clusters a story across outlets and links publishers.

MyNews beats Ground News on transparency, not on rater count: it aggregates AllSides, Ad Fontes, and MBFC, always cites which org produced each number, and shows their disagreement instead of asserting one in-house verdict. Bias lives on two separate axes (political lean and reliability/factuality), source ratings rate the outlet and never tint an article body, and a Blindspot surfacer (seeded from aggregated bias data, refined by bridging-based community notes) shows what a reader's usual feed under-covers.

Cohort 4: Open protocol and open journalism

Bluesky is the strongest proof that user-controlled algorithms and composable moderation scale without a token: 40M-plus users on the AT Protocol, 50,000-plus custom feeds, and a $100M Series B (Bain Capital Crypto plus Knight Foundation) in April 2025 (Social Media Today, Gigazine). Nostr shows a censorship-resistant relay model monetized by Bitcoin Lightning rather than equity. Mastodon (ActivityPub, under 1M monthly actives after its post-Musk spike, restructuring into a European nonprofit on roughly a $5.1M budget) shows federation's funding fragility. On the credibility and open-science side, ProPublica (nonprofit, $64.3M revenue in 2024, nine Pulitzers) and The Conversation (academics write, editors edit, CC-licensed, funded by 200-plus universities, read 40M-plus times a month, republished in 90 countries) are the durable models for trustworthy, reusable journalism and science.

This cohort is MyNews's actual architecture, but it does not need a third-party protocol: Meerkat already implements the same primitives natively (signed cm_posts, the Plan 19 signed publication path to a public/forever archive, the ranked-choice mesh transport ladder, and swappable moderation labelers). MyNews adds an article/preprint post_type and an open-science metadata sidecar rather than rebuilding signing, sync, archive, or moderation. The nonprofit half mirrors ProPublica and The Conversation: a 501(c)(3) running diamond open access, in-app nonprofit donations, and grants (Press Forward alone is deploying $500M-plus), which app revenue could never match.

Insight No single competitor fuses the two halves MyNews proposes: user-controlled, transparent feeds (Bluesky's territory), open CC-licensed science and journalism (The Conversation and ProPublica's territory), a bias/perspective layer (Ground News's territory), and individual-journalist trust (the creator-subscription territory), all without a speculative token. Each capability is proven somewhere; the whitespace is the combination, and the locked direction lets MyNews assemble it on one owned substrate (Meerkat / @mylife/sync) instead of stitching four external systems together.
Verdict Reuse rails, do not invent or rent them. Internally MyNews rides the Meerkat substrate for identity, signed posts, mesh sync, the Plan 19 archive, and moderation hooks; externally it uses creator-subscription economics, a Ground-News-style multi-source bias spectrum, and nonprofit / diamond-OA funding for the science half. It ships as a standalone apps/mynews app plus a paired modules/mynews hub module, monetized with a $4.99 Manhattan-pattern unlock plus web-routed tips plus MyLife Pro inclusion, and it avoids the one thing every casualty in cohort one shared: a bespoke token.

Why Now: Demand Reality

The demand case for MyNews is strong on the problem and historically weak on the wallet. The pain (collapsed trust, relentless negativity, loss of feed control) is population-scale and growing. Willingness to pay for a standalone news app is genuinely poor. Both facts are true at once. The founder has chosen a $4.99 one-time unlock anyway, and the locked design answers the wallet problem not by abandoning that price but by de-risking it: reading is free on both surfaces so reach and the journalist-trust flywheel are never gated, the $4.99 unlocks only the power layer, tips are always on, MyLife Pro bundles it, and a 501(c)(3) funds the open-science half.

The trust collapse is real and durable

Gallup's 2025 reading put US adults expressing a great deal or fair amount of trust in mass media at 28%, below 2024's 31% record low and under 30% for the first time in the poll's roughly 50-year history, with 70% now saying "not very much" or "none at all." Pew (September 2025) shows trust in national news organizations at 56%, down 20 points since 2016, while local news holds near 70 to 74%. Reuters Institute's 2025 report finds global trust in news stalled at 40% for a third straight year. This is a grievance the size of the population, not a niche complaint. Crucially, trust in news encountered through third parties is far lower than trust in news overall (roughly 32% via search, 22% via social, 20% via AI answers), which is exactly the gap a transparent, user-controlled feed targets, and exactly why MyNews ships a non-profiling default feed and follow-a-journalist for free, with the verified-plus-pseudonymous track record visible to every reader before any paywall.

Negativity and avoidance map onto the pitch

MyNews is not hard to prototype. It is hard to make honest. Six problems sit at the center, and the locked direction is built specifically to route around the demo-grade version of each: a standalone apps/mynews Expo app plus a paired modules/mynews hub module over the Meerkat @mylife/sync substrate, free reading on both surfaces with a $4.99 power unlock, individual journalists (verified and pseudonymous) as the trust spine, and Ground-News-style bias badges instead of whole-page coloring. The founder's original instinct to tint the whole page by political lean was reshaped into discrete badges plus a spectrum bar plus source and ownership context, for three concrete reasons worth recording. Granularity: four of the five major systems rate the source, not the article. 3. The free feed must broaden, not narrow

Reuters Institute records selective news avoidance at 40% globally, up from 29% in 2017, driven mainly by negative impact on mood. Building and saving the algorithm-web is paid; reading and broadening stay free.

4. "Decentralized" cannot be the canonical scientific record

A purely mesh-first architecture breaks the science mission. Persistent DOIs are a governance commitment by centralized registries, and a registered DataCite DOI cannot simply be deleted (DataCite); IPFS explicitly does not guarantee permanence, since unpinned content is garbage-collected (IPFS docs); even decentralized-science projects attach DataCite DOIs and HTTP resolvers for backwards compatibility (DeSci). This is precisely why MyNews reuses the Meerkat substrate's hybrid model rather than rebuilding one. Public reach is reached only via Plan 19's signed PublicationDescriptor writing a separate cp_publications row to published_blob, so a draft can never silently escalate; the server-backed canonical record carries DOI registration via Crossref/DataCite and retraction authority; and mesh plus device-local layers are draft store, offline cache, and optimistic sync only. The dependency is stated honestly: honest public reach cannot turn on until the Plan 20 hosted relay (DEFAULT_RELAY_URL is empty today) and at least one always-on seed host land.

5. Verification proves affiliation, not competence

The verified journalist tier uses claimable bylines plus optional affiliation proof via domain email, ORCID for science authorship (roughly 2.9M records carry a verified affiliation, ORCID), or manual review for at-risk reporters who cannot expose a domain email. The load-bearing caveat, shown in the UI: verification proves WHERE someone works or that a byline is genuinely theirs, NOT that they are right, so the verified badge is kept visually and semantically separate from the accuracy reputation. The open pseudonymous tier is first-class, not second: a persistent handle backed by key continuity earns trust purely through a track record plus community notes, and a long-running pseudonymous reporter with a correction-honest record can out-rank a freshly verified one. Reputation accrues to both tiers from signed, immutable events (corrections that can improve standing when handled transparently, accuracy history, community-note hit rate, peer endorsements, and a PREreview-style ladder for science) and is portable because it is keyed to the journalist's Ed25519 identity, so a media owner cannot strip it.

Publishing flow and open science

Why hybrid is forced, not chosen

A purely peer-to-peer or device-local architecture cannot be the canonical scientific record. Recommended layered architecture

  • L0 Identity: reuse MyLife Ed25519/X25519 device and agent keypairs for a real "export and re-host your account" exit. For science reviewers, layer ORCID sign-in plus institutional SSO plus verified-email Trust Markers (affiliation, not subject-matter expertise).

  • L5 Composable feeds: named feed graphs that emit an ordered skeleton of item IDs, hydrated separately, so feeds are portable and shareable.
  • L6 Composable moderation: one opinionated default Ozone-style labeler plus third-party labelers users subscribe to, plus bridging-ranked community notes.

The trust spine: individual journalists, two tiers

Outlets are not the unit of trust; the byline is. Both tiers accrue a per-journalist track record (nw_journalist_track) computed from signed, immutable events, and both are payout-relevant. Neither is second-class: a long-running pseudonymous reporter with a correction-honest record can out-rank a freshly verified one.

Verified tier

Claimable bylines with an earned, optional badge. Affiliation proof via domain email (verified via nytimes.com), ORCID for science authorship, or manual review for at-risk reporters who cannot expose a domain email. Every verification shows its method and date so readers can judge it. Follow-a-journalist feeds and a public corrections history.

Open pseudonymous tier

A persistent handle backed by Ed25519 key continuity, no identity disclosure required. Trust is earned purely through track record plus community notes. A pseudonymous account can still earn a consistent-handle / key-continuity marker (proving the same person over time) without ever naming itself, which protects sources and at-risk reporters.

Verification proves where, not whether. A badge proves where someone works or that a byline is genuinely theirs; it does not prove they are right. So the verified badge is kept visually and semantically separate from the accuracy reputation. Corrections are first-class and can improve standing when handled transparently (logged via cm_post_lifecycle draft/published/retracted), which rewards the honest-correction behavior that actually builds trust. Reputation is portable because it is keyed to the journalist's keypair and exportable, so a media owner cannot strip a reporter's record.

Bias: Ground-News-style, two axes, never tinted

The explicit goal is to emulate and beat Ground News. nw_bias_rating caches source/author ratings on two separate axes (political lean and reliability/factuality), aggregated across AllSides, Ad Fontes, and Media Bias/Fact Check, with coverage-from-all-sides clustering and a nw_blindspot surfacer that shows what your usual feed under-covers, seeded from the aggregated data and refined by bridging notes. The differentiator over Ground News (which reportedly leans on third-party raters it does not pay) is owned, transparent methodology: always cite which org produced each number and show their disagreement rather than asserting one verdict. MBFC is the weakest input (Poynter called its method "in no way scientific"), which is exactly why surfacing rater divergence beats laundering a single score.

Why hybrid, not pure mesh

A purely device-local or peer-to-peer architecture cannot be the canonical scientific record. Persistent DOIs resolve through the centrally governed Handle System run by registration agencies like Crossref and DataCite, and a registered DOI cannot simply be deleted (DataCite, DOI.org). Durable archiving has the same constraint: IPFS explicitly does not guarantee permanence, content survives only while a node pins it (IPFS docs). So a server-backed canonical record plus harm moderation runs server-side, and mesh stays a draft store, offline cache, and optimistic-sync layer, matching MyLife's transport-honesty rule and the BestChef public-launch exception. AT Protocol is the right reference for the consumer half (portable identity, composable moderation, no token); its cheapest piece is genuinely cheap, with a full-network relay run on a roughly $34/month VPS (whtwnd), but its expensive AppView indexer degrades near 100,000 users on a naive Postgres fan-in (bitcrowd). The replacement keeps the same intent (make leaning legible at a glance) with accessible, global-safe execution: a neutral surface, two separate axes (reliability and lean), a multi-encoded badge, a "this rates the outlet, not this article" disclaimer, coverage-from-all-sides distribution, and a Blindspot surfacer. The differentiator that beats Ground News (which reportedly relies on third-party raters it does not pay) is transparent owned methodology: aggregate AllSides, Ad Fontes, and MBFC, always cite which org produced each number, and show their disagreement rather than asserting one verdict.

Trust spine: verified plus pseudonymous journalists

Individual journalists, not outlets, are the trust unit, in two first-class tiers. The verified tier has claimable bylines, an earned and optional badge, optional identity/affiliation verification (domain email, ORCID for science authorship, or manual review for at-risk journalists), and follow-a-journalist feeds. The open pseudonymous tier is a persistent handle backed by key continuity with no identity disclosure, earning trust purely through track record plus community notes. Crucially, verification proves where someone works or that a byline is genuinely theirs, not that they are right, so the verified badge is kept visually and semantically separate from the accuracy reputation, and a long-running pseudonymous reporter with a correction-honest record can out-rank a freshly verified one. ORCID is well-suited here: about 2.9 million records carry an affiliation, which proves institutional belonging but not subject-matter expertise, so a PREreview-style track-record ladder sits on top (ORCID). Reputation is computed from signed, immutable events (corrections, accuracy history via cm_post_lifecycle, note hit rate, endorsements), corrections can improve standing when handled transparently, and the record is portable because it is tied to the journalist's keypair.

Moderation pipeline: layered, never a single mechanism

Reuse the Meerkat moderation hooks. Tier 1 is a swappable advisory ML triage classifier behind an interface (do not hard-depend on Perspective API, reportedly sunsetting Dec 31 2026). Tier 2 is one opinionated default Ozone-style labeler plus composable third-party labelers users subscribe to (stackable moderation). Tier 3 is bridging-ranked community notes (open-source matrix factorization, polarity-independent intercept around 0.40) that feed both per-article notes and the Blindspot surfacer. Bridging cannot be the launch layer: only about 5 to 9% of notes are ever shown and English publication fell from 9.5% in 2023 to 4.9% in early 2025 (DDIA), median time to a verdict runs 10 to 26 hours (health-notes study, 4-year study), and a coordinated 5 to 20% of raters can shift outcomes (Truong et al. 2025), so the default labeler plus ML triage carry day one. Legal, Safety and Policy

MyNews ships as two surfaces over one core (the standalone apps/mynews Expo app and the paired modules/mynews hub module), so the legal floor is built once in the shared MyLife/Meerkat shell and inherited by both. The category is the most legally exposed an app can occupy: user-written news and opinion (defamation and election-law risk), redistributed scientific papers (copyright risk), and ranked, summarized content (Section 230 erosion risk), all gated by Apple and Google moderation minimums for user-generated content. Reusing the Meerkat substrate means MyNews does not re-certify signing, sync, archive, or moderation; it inherits the shell's compliance plumbing.

Verdict None of the legal plumbing is deferrable, and free reading does not exempt it. App-store reviewers reject UGC apps that lack a pre-publication filter, in-app reporting, user blocking, and a published contact, and a lapsed DMCA agent voids the entire copyright safe harbor. Because reading is free and ungated on both surfaces, more eyeballs hit the moderation pipeline sooner, not later, so the compliance floor must ship before the first public post.

US intermediary liability: curation is the danger zone

Section 230 still immunizes the platform for defamatory or false articles written by users, and courts still protect traditional editorial functions (deciding whether to publish, remove, or format) so long as edits do not substantially alter meaning. The risk for MyNews is precisely its value proposition. In Anderson v. TikTok (2024) the Third Circuit held that an algorithmic recommendation feed is the platform's own expressive activity and is not immunized (Congress CRS R46751). A recommender, an AI summary, or an editor-written headline can convert MyNews from immunized host into co-author of a libel. This is why the reused Meerkat substrate defaults to chronological and labeled surfaces, the free tier ships a non-profiling feed, the platform never authors headlines, and AI summaries are attributed to their source outlet and never alter an article's meaning.

The 2025 TAKE IT DOWN Act adds a federal, non-230 obligation the FTC has enforced since 19 May 2026: any covered UGC platform must offer a removal-request path and take down non-consensual intimate imagery (including AI deepfakes) plus identical copies within 48 hours, with civil penalties cited around $53,088 per violation, regardless of company size.

App-store rules are the gating constraint

Apple Guideline 1.2 requires four things of any UGC app: pre-publication filtering of objectionable material, a report mechanism with timely response, the ability to block abusive users, and published contact info (App Store Review Guidelines). Google Play's UGC policy is parallel and adds in-app reporting for AI-generated content, and removes apps that primarily host objectionable UGC (Google Play policy). Both require an enforced EULA with zero-tolerance terms accepted before the first post. Apple's 2025 age-rating overhaul pushes unmoderated UGC to 16+ or higher, and a November 2025 change can require age-assurance for content above the app's rating. The standalone app and the hub module inherit one shared shell implementation of all four controls (the UGC four), so there is no second code path to certify and no drift between surfaces.

Copyright and the open-science line

The line for the science half is sharp. Hosting a publisher's Version of Record PDF is infringement: the Sci-Hub model (Elsevier won a $15M US judgment and a permanent injunction; ACS won an injunction reaching ISPs and search engines) and the line ResearchGate was sued over (a 2022 Munich court found it liable; publishers settled in 2023 only after ResearchGate added automated rights checks at the point of upload). The defensible path is the opposite: host author preprints, Author Accepted Manuscripts deposited under the cOAlition S Rights Retention Strategy, and natively CC BY (gold/diamond) articles where the author retained rights, or link out to the DOI (cOAlition S, open-access.network). The NIH Public Access Policy widened this pool: since 1 July 2025 NIH-funded AAMs must be deposited in PubMed Central with zero embargo. But RRS faces publisher pushback, and the OSTP Nelson-memo zero-embargo push is reportedly being repealed (copyright.princeton.edu), so do not build on it. The Phase 0 automated rights check enforces this per upload (route, version, signed agreement), accepting only preprint, AAM-RRS, CC BY, or gold, never the VoR PDF. To keep DMCA Section 512 safe harbor, register a designated agent with the Copyright Office ($6, renew every 3 years), publish the contact, run notice-and-takedown, and enforce a repeat-infringer termination policy (copyright.gov/512).

EU DSA, elections, and source protection

The EU Digital Services Act applies if MyNews has any EU users. Even as a micro or small enterprise (the heavier Chapter III Section 3 duties are waived under Article 19), MyNews owes the hosting baseline: Article 16 notice-and-action, Article 17 statements of reasons, clear terms, and a published legal point of contact (digitalservicesact.cc). Article 27 separately requires disclosing recommender parameters in plain language and offering at least one non-profiling feed, which the free default feed satisfies by construction. Election law argues against an automated political-truth censor: roughly 30 states regulate election deepfakes mostly via disclosure after a federal judge struck California's AB 2839 and gutted AB 2655 in 2025 on First Amendment and Section 230 grounds (Brennan Center), so the safe posture is AI-disclosure labels plus advisory community-note flags, never platform-authored verdicts. There is no federal journalist shield (the PRESS Act died in the Senate in December 2024), so MyNews cannot promise source protection against federal subpoenas, though about 49 states have shield laws or recognized privilege (rcfp.org). This matters for the open pseudonymous journalist tier: the platform protects identity through key continuity, not through a legal privilege it does not have.

The three regulatory landmines

Section 230 erosion via curation

The Third Circuit's Anderson v. TikTok (2024) held that an algorithmic recommendation feed is the platform's own expressive activity, outside 230 (CRS R46751). Ranked feeds, AI summaries, and platform-written headlines can convert MyNews from immunized host to co-author of a libel, and outside the US there is no 230 equivalent. Default to chronological/labeled surfaces, attribute AI summaries to source outlets, and never let the platform author headlines.

TAKE IT DOWN Act (FTC-enforced since May 19 2026)

Every covered UGC platform needs an NCII removal path and must take down non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI deepfakes, plus identical copies within 48 hours. Not 230-waivable, not size-exempt, and the FTC has already sent warning letters (FTC). Stand up the request form, 48h SLA, and identical-copy hash matching as shared-shell infrastructure before US launch.

App-store UGC minimums

Apple Guideline 1.2 requires a pre-publication filter, in-app report with timely response, block-user, and published contact (Apple); Google Play mirrors this and adds in-app reporting for AI-generated content (Google). Free reading and mesh delivery do not exempt the submitted app. Build the UGC four into the shared shell or the whole super-app gets rejected on first review.

Naming and Branding

Under the locked direction, MyNews is a standalone app in the MyLife family: its own Expo app at apps/mynews that ships to the App Store and Play Store as "MyNews", plus a paired hub module at modules/mynews that appears as a card on the MyLife dashboard exactly like the other modules. Insight The trust data favors a primary-source framing over an aggregator framing. Local and primary-source news still draws roughly 70 to 74% trust while national news organizations sit near 56% (Pew, Sept 2025). MyNews answers this not through the name but through the product: the trust spine is individual journalists (a verified tier and an open pseudonymous tier), so the brand reads as "the people you trust by what they have actually published and gotten right", which is the more-trusted category that an outlet-level aggregator name cannot claim.

Recommendation: ship as MyNews on both surfaces

The locked packaging decision resolves the prior split between a hub label and a separate product brand: MyNews is the name on the App Store listing, the Play Store listing, and the hub dashboard card, because the standalone app and the hub module are two views of one product and must read as the same thing to the same user. Clear MyNews for trademark before design spend; MyWire is the lower-risk fallback inside the My- family, and Clarion is the strongest mark if the founder ever wants to break the family convention (paired with a "by MyLife" descriptor). Prism is crowded and should be dropped.

Brand visual rule: neutral chrome, badges not wallpaper

The founder's original idea of coloring the whole page by political leaning was reshaped into discrete badges plus a spectrum bar plus source and ownership context, for three concrete reasons worth recording. Accessibility: WCAG Success Criterion 1.4.1 (Level A) forbids color as the only means of conveying information, and roughly 1 in 12 men have color vision deficiency. Internationalization: the red/blue partisan mapping is a post-2000 US convention that is inverted across most of the world (red signals the left, blue the right in the UK and Europe), so a globally distributed app that floods the page with one color reads as confusing or as taking a side (red and blue states, political colour). Neutrality and correctness: source ratings rate the outlet, not the individual article, and rater divergence concentrates in exactly the center-rated outlets a tinted page would most often mislabel, so a whole-page tint would look partisan and be wrong.

The replacement is a Ground-News-style signal on a neutral MyLife/Cool Obsidian surface: bias as a discrete, dismissible, multi-encoded chip carrying text and shape (never color alone), on two separate axes (reliability and political lean), with a "this rates the outlet, not this article" disclaimer, and always citing which rating organization produced each number, never a page tint.

LeftCenterRightScience

Labeled chips like these keep the interface neutral while still making leaning legible at a glance: the signal is a badge, not the wallpaper. This is the same execution that powers the Blindspot surfacer and the coverage-from-all-sides view, so the brand and the bias model share one visual language across the standalone app and the hub module.

North star A news and open-science platform where you own your identity, compose and save your own feeds, choose which journalists and raters you trust, and read a verifiable, primary-source-linked record that no single media owner controls. MyNews is the name; the promise is that you trust people, not outlets.

Aggregating AllSides, Ad Fontes, and MBFC at the source level is locked, but AllSides and MBFC told CJR there is no formal agreement or compensation with Ground News, so licensing terms and cost are unknown. Resolve license-vs-build and article-level-vs-source-level scope before Phase 3.
  • DOI registration agency. Choose Crossref or DataCite for persistent identifiers and budget membership plus per-DOI fees. This is required for the science record to be citable and is part of the always-available coordination layer a pure mesh network cannot provide.
  • Behavior-change claim hygiene. Do not market bias comparison as a mind-changer. The primary study (Bram, PLOS One 2024) found exposure shifts opinion only conditionally, when a story violates partisan expectations, on a small unrepresentative sample. Treat "see all sides" as an acquisition hook and an instrumented breadth-of-exposure guardrail, not a proven conversion lever.
