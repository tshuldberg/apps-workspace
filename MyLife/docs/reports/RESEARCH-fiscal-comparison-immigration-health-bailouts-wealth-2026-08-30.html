2. Immigration: the requested status split is not observable

There is no federal ledger that assigns every tax receipt, school cost, hospital payment, public good, or citizen child's benefit to a legal or unauthorized adult. Naturalized citizens, permanent residents, humanitarian entrants, temporary visa holders, pending applicants, and mixed-status families also prevent a clean binary. CBO's recent "other foreign nationals" category includes people with and without lawful status.

International bank-rescue evidence

Eurostat's 2007-2025 series reports cumulative bank-rescue deficit effects above 10% of 2025 GDP in Cyprus and Greece, 5% to 10% in Ireland, Portugal, and Slovenia, and 1% to 5% in Spain, Latvia, Austria, and Germany. Luxembourg, Denmark, Belgium, Sweden, and France recorded cumulative net gains after fees, interest, dividends, and asset sales. At the end of 2025, bank-rescue-related government debt stood at EUR383.4B, or 2.0% of EU GDP, with EUR54.4B of contingent liabilities.

6. Returns with adjusted gross income above $5 million

The IRS reports returns, not unique people. This review combines the $5M-under-$10M and $10M-or-more bins. Adjusted gross income excludes unrealized appreciation, so it is not a measure of annual wealth growth.

Scale conclusion: The annual U.S. health-care system is roughly two orders of magnitude larger than the strongest annual state-local immigration net-cost estimate and far larger than the final lifetime cost of TARP. This is a statement about scale, not a judgment about policy value.

  1. Immigration cannot be split into a defensible national legal-versus-unauthorized total. Recent federal and local effects differ in sign because they cover different taxes and services.
  2. Health care is the dominant recurring flow and the U.S. is the high-income-country outlier.
  3. Bailout headlines overstate cost when they substitute gross credit for net fiscal loss.
  4. Corporate legislative scores and annual tax expenditures use different baselines and overlap.
  5. Above-$5M IRS data support a reproducible realized-income estimate, but not a complete valuation of tax advantages or unrealized wealth.
