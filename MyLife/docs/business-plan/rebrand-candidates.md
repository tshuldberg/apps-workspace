# Rebrand Candidates

**Date:** 2026-03-22
**Context:** "MyLife" undersells the product vision. It sounds like a lifestyle blog, not a personal data operating system. We need a name that conveys sovereignty, power, privacy, and comprehensiveness.

**Methodology:** 12 candidates brainstormed around themes of data sovereignty, anti-enshittification, unified personal OS, privacy as power, and individual empowerment. Each checked against App Store conflicts, domain availability, and trademark risk.

**Domain reality:** All single-word .com domains for real English words are registered. The strategy for any candidate is: (1) try .app TLD (purpose-built for apps, HTTPS-enforced), (2) try `get[name].com` or `[name]hq.com`, (3) consider acquisition of the .com if budget allows.

## 1. Bastion

**Tagline:** Your life, fortified.

**Domains:**
- `bastion.com` -- Taken (registered 1995, Namecheap, paid through 2034)
- `bastion.app` -- Taken (DNS resolves)
- `bastion.io` -- Taken (registered 2013)
- `getbastion.com` -- Taken (registered 2020)
- `bastionhq.com` -- Taken (registered Jan 2026)
- `lifebastion.com` -- **AVAILABLE**

**App Store conflicts:** Bastion by Supergiant Games (acclaimed 2011 indie video game, 2M+ downloads). BASTION APP (luxury pen/watch accessories). Neither are personal life management apps.

**Trademark risk:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Supergiant Games holds a Class 9 (downloadable software) trademark for "Bastion" covering video game software. Different product category (games vs. personal productivity) provides some distance, but same trademark class. Would require legal review.

**Pros:**
- Immediately evocative: a bastion is a fortified place, a last stronghold. Perfect metaphor for privacy-first software in the enshittification era.
- One syllable. Easy to spell, easy to say, easy to remember. "Just download Bastion."
- Works at every scale: indie app, funded startup, or household brand.

**Cons:**
- Supergiant Games' Bastion is beloved in the gaming community. Some brand confusion among gamers.
- .com and .app both taken. Best available domain is `lifebastion.com`, which dilutes the brand.

## 2. Sovereign

**Tagline:** Your data answers to you.

**Domains:**
- `sovereign.com` -- Taken (registered 1995, Gandi)
- `sovereign.app` -- Taken (DNS resolves)
- `sovereign.io` -- Taken (registered 2015)
- `usesovereign.com` -- Taken (registered 2024)

**App Store conflicts:** Sovereign Global (wealth management), Sovereign Plus (payroll), Sovereign Bank (banking), Sovereign Lifestyle (fitness), Sovereign Development (real estate). None are personal life management apps, but the name is well-used in finance.

**Trademark risk:** LOW-MEDIUM. "Sovereign" is widely used across industries (finance, crypto, identity) but no dominant single player in the personal software space. "Self-sovereign identity" is a growing movement, which could help or hinder.

**Pros:**
- Says exactly what the product promises: data sovereignty. The name IS the brand positioning.
- Resonates with the privacy/degoogle/self-hosted community (Reddit r/privacy, r/selfhosted, r/degoogle -- the target audience).
- Three syllables with weight. "Sovereign" sounds important, which it should.

**Cons:**
- Can sound grandiose or political. Some users might associate it with the "sovereign citizen" movement, which carries negative connotations.
- Domain landscape is bleak. No clean .com or .app available without a prefix.

## Top 5 Ranking

### 1. Bastion

**Why #1:** Best overall balance. The fortress metaphor communicates both privacy (protection) and comprehensiveness (everything inside the walls). One syllable, instantly memorable, works at every scale. The Supergiant Games conflict is real but manageable -- different product categories, and their game is 15 years old. The brand upside is enormous: "Bastion" sounds like something worth paying for, something built to last, something that protects you.

**Domain strategy:** Register `lifebastion.com` (available) immediately. Pursue `bastion.app` acquisition. Long-term: negotiate for `bastion.com`.

### 2. Holdfast

**Why #2:** The most distinctive name on this list. No one else has it. The sailor's knuckle tattoo origin story is marketing gold. "Hold fast to what is yours" is the anti-enshittification manifesto in four words. `holdfast.app` appears to be available, giving a clean primary domain. The risk is that it requires brand education -- people won't know what the product does from the name alone. But that is true of Slack, Notion, and every other successful productivity brand.

**Domain strategy:** Register `holdfast.app` immediately if available. Consider `holdfastlife.com` as backup.

### 3. Bulwark

**Why #3:** Cleanest competitive landscape of any candidate. No tech company, no funded startup, no popular app uses this name. `bulwark.app` appears available. The metaphor is on point: a bulwark is a defensive wall, and this product is a wall between your personal data and the companies that want to monetize it. The risk is that "bulwark" is an unfamiliar word for younger or non-native English speakers. But unfamiliar words become familiar when attached to products people love (Hulu, Roku, Canva).

**Domain strategy:** Register `bulwark.app` immediately if available. Consider `getbulwark.com` as backup.

### 4. Sovereign

**Why #4:** The most ideologically aligned name. If the brand is "data sovereignty," then the name should say it. The privacy/degoogle/self-hosted community will immediately understand and champion this name. The risk is the "sovereign citizen" association and the slight grandiosity of the word. But in context -- "Sovereign: Your data answers to you" -- the meaning is clear and powerful. Domain landscape is the weakest of the top 5.

**Domain strategy:** Explore `sovereign.app` acquisition. Register creative alternatives like `sovereignapp.com` or `getsovereign.com` if available.

### 5. Atrium

**Why #5:** The most approachable name on this list. An atrium is a welcoming, central, light-filled space. It communicates the hub concept without aggression. If the go-to-market targets mainstream consumers (not just privacy enthusiasts), Atrium's neutrality is a strength. The risk is that it doesn't carry the anti-enshittification edge. But not every user cares about that fight -- some just want one app for everything. Atrium serves them without alienating the privacy crowd.

**Domain strategy:** Pursue `atrium.app` acquisition (likely parked). Register `getatrium.com` or `atrium.life` if available.

## Decision Framework

The right name depends on which audience you prioritize first:

| Audience | Best name | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| Privacy/degoogle community (early adopters) | **Sovereign** | Speaks their language directly |
| Tech-savvy productivity users | **Bastion** | Powerful but approachable |
| Mainstream consumers | **Atrium** or **Hearth** | Warm, inviting, no jargon |
| Anti-corporate/anti-enshittification movement | **Bulwark** or **Holdfast** | The name IS the fight |

**Recommended primary:** **Bastion**. It has the widest appeal across all four audiences, the strongest single-word brand potential, and a metaphor that works for both the privacy pitch ("your data is fortified") and the convenience pitch ("everything inside one stronghold").

**Recommended runner-up:** **Holdfast**. If you want to lean harder into the anti-enshittification identity and create a brand that is unmistakably different from every productivity app on the market, Holdfast is the one. It is polarizing in the best way -- people who get it will love it, and people who love it will evangelize it.
