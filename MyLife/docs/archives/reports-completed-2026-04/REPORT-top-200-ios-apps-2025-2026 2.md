- MAU = Monthly Active Users
- Est. = Estimate based on available data

## Key Takeaways for MyLife Strategy

1. **Privacy is the moat.** The biggest apps (Flo, MyFitnessPal, Strava, Duolingo) are all data-hungry. MyLife's offline-first, zero-telemetry approach is a genuine differentiator, especially in health, finance, and personal tracking.

2. **Fragmentation creates opportunity.** Users currently need 15-20 separate apps to cover what MyLife bundles into one hub. The "app fatigue" factor is real -- nobody wants 20 subscriptions.

3. **Subscription fatigue is acute.** Adding up the subscriptions for YNAB ($109/yr) + Strava ($80/yr) + Calm ($70/yr) + AllTrails ($36/yr) + 1Password ($36/yr) + Notion ($96/yr) = $427/yr for 6 apps. MyLife Pro as a single subscription covering 29 modules is compelling.

4. **Biggest competitive gaps (smallest incumbents):**
   - Closet/wardrobe management (~4M total market)
   - Journal/mood tracking (~3-5M market)
   - Subscription tracking (~4M market)
   - Pet care tracking (~3M for tracking apps)
   - Car maintenance tracking (~10M fragmented)
   - RSVP/event planning (emerging, Apple entering)

5. **Hardest modules to compete on (massive incumbents):**
   - Notes (Notion 100M, Apple Notes built-in)
   - Nutrition (MyFitnessPal 220M)
   - Language/words (Duolingo 133M)
   - Cycle tracking (Flo 77M, Clue 100M)
   - Trails (AllTrails 60M)

6. **Apple's built-in threat:** Apple Health, Apple Weather, Apple Maps, Apple Notes, Screen Time, Shortcuts, Apple Invites (new), and Apple Calendar cover significant ground. MyLife's advantage is deeper feature sets and cross-module integration.
