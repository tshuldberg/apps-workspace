The gap in the market

Why no one has built this

Privacy failure is now priced in

Flo Health settled with the FTC after sharing menstrual data with Facebook and Google. Strava's heatmap exposed military bases. Life360 sold precise location data to the Arity ad broker. Users are pricing in the risk and regulators are catching up.

The stack costs $500+/yr

Strava ($80) + MyFitnessPal ($80) + YNAB ($109) + Flo ($50) + Calm ($70) + AllTrails ($36) + Day One ($35) + Quizlet ($48) cost $508/yr stacked. Every incumbent is single-category by construction. That bundling gap is Meerkat.

Cross-module intelligence is structural

Workouts correlate with mood. Cycle correlates with sleep. Nutrition correlates with energy. On stacked single-purpose apps these insights are impossible. On one database they are trivial. No competitor can replicate this without merging with 38 others.

Incumbents are legally unable to compete on price

Public companies have fiduciary obligations to maximize shareholder returns. VC-backed competitors need high ARPU to justify their valuations. Ad-funded apps have contractual revenue dependencies on data brokers they cannot walk away from. Rollup acquirers paid billions and must extract value to recoup. None of them can drop to $12/yr without destroying their business model. Meerkat has no ads, no data broker contracts, no investor pressure to inflate pricing, and minimal infrastructure overhead since core data lives on-device. We are structurally free to price where they cannot follow.

The privacy and cost advantage
📱

No servers required

Your phone talks directly to your other devices over Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or the internet. Meerkat never stores your data on our servers. This is how iMessage and AirDrop work, applied to your entire personal life.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Share without an account

Families share grocery lists, budgets, and home notes directly between phones. No cloud account, no monthly server bill, no data broker in the middle. Works at a dinner table with the router off.

💰

Near-zero marginal cost

Every competitor runs expensive per-user cloud infrastructure. Meerkat keeps core data on-device with servers only for optional social features. Our infrastructure costs are a fraction of theirs at every scale.

Decentralized mesh sync is the moat

Every competitor is server-first. Meerkat is phone-first: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, then internet, then an encrypted-only relay as a last resort. Friends, families, and communities share data without anyone's server in the middle. This architecture is structurally hard for ad-funded and rollup-owned incumbents to ship. It took years to build, and it is the reason our margins are fundamentally different from every company in the competitive globe above.
