# Bobby — Deep Dive (vs MySubs)

**Module:** subs
**Tier:** direct (indie pure-play subscription tracker)
**Founded:** 2017
**HQ:** Amsterdam, Netherlands
**Status:** private (indie, solo / small team)

## One-line
The minimalist, beautifully designed subscription tracker that avoids bank integration entirely — a quiet indie app with Apple Design Award credentials and a devoted Reddit following as the "private Rocket Money."

## Product
- Manual-entry subscription list with icon per service (icon library is a key feature)
- Renewal calendar with push notifications before charge
- Monthly/yearly cost rollup, currency conversion
- iCloud sync across devices
- Widgets (iOS home screen)
- **Platforms:** iOS (primary), Android
- **Pricing:** Free tier (up to ~5 subscriptions); $2.99 one-time IAP to unlock unlimited + all features
- **Distinguishing UX:** the prettiest sub tracker — icon-forward, glanceable, zero bank linking

## How they make money (precise)
Primary: one-time $2.99 IAP. Secondary: none. Zero ads, zero analytics, zero data sales. Indie bootstrapped economics — App Store is distribution, word-of-mouth drives growth.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **Manual entry is tedious at scale:** 30+ subscriptions takes 15+ min of data entry. MyLife MySubs inherits same limitation but roadmap includes opt-in bank-payee detection.
- **No cross-module context:** Bobby shows "you spend $X/mo on subs" but doesn't tie to budget. MyLife connects to MyBudget for unified view.
- **Weak Android:** Bobby is iOS-first; Android version lags. MyLife ships parity.
- **No price alternative flagging:** Bobby just lists; MyLife can flag cheaper alternatives.
- **Bobby is an ally more than a threat:** both share the "no bank linking" ethos. Real threat is Rocket Money's scale, which MyLife competes with via suite bundling.
