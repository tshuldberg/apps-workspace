# Polarsteps — Deep Dive (vs MyTravel)

**Module:** travel
**Tier:** direct
**Founded:** 2014
**HQ:** Amsterdam, Netherlands
**Status:** private (profitable, bootstrapped after early seed)

## One-line
The long-term traveler's auto-tracking journal — runs in background, records every place you visit, and ships a hardcover travel book at the end, with a photo-book commerce engine as the unique monetization angle.

## Product
- Automatic GPS tracking of visited places (background)
- Photo + note journal per "step" on a trip
- Public/private trip sharing
- Stats (countries visited, distance, time)
- Printed travel books (the commerce engine)
- Platforms: iOS, Android, Web
- Pricing: Free tier; Premium €29.99/yr; travel books €39.99-€89.99 per book
- Distinguishing UX choice: hardcover printed book of your trip mailed to your door — physical keepsake as moat

## How they make money (precise)
1. **Printed travel books** (€39.99-€89.99): the cash engine; customers spend >€50 per completed trip on average.
2. **Premium subscription** (€29.99/yr): unlimited photo uploads, offline maps, extended stats.
3. Minor affiliate links to gear/insurance.

Unit economics: print-on-demand has COGS (paper, shipping) ~40-55% of book price; net margin per book still strong. Subs are >75% gross margin. Commerce mix yields a healthier LTV than pure subs.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **Background GPS always on**: Polarsteps' product design requires constant location tracking. MyTravel is manual-entry-first; location data stays on-device even when recording is enabled.
- **Printed-book anchor**: Polarsteps needs you to care about physical keepsakes. MyTravel's journal + memory tabs serve the same emotional goal without requiring commerce.
- **No logistics layer**: no flights/booking parsing, no packing integration. MyTravel has logistics + bucket list + trips integrated.
- **Public-feed gravity**: Polarsteps has a public trip feed; many users feel pressured to perform. MyTravel is private-by-default.
