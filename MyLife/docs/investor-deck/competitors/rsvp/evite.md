# Evite — Deep Dive (vs MyRSVP)

**Module:** rsvp
**Tier:** direct
**Founded:** 1998
**HQ:** West Hollywood, CA
**Status:** private (owned by Francisco Partners / PE)

## One-line
The pioneer of digital invitations, now pivoting from ad-supported to premium + affiliate model after 26 years of on/off profitability under multiple PE owners.

## Product
- Digital invitation maker with 20,000+ designs, custom + licensed (Disney/Marvel/Sesame)
- RSVP tracking, guest messaging, gift registry, donation requests
- Premium themes, video invites, no-ads upgrade
- Group gifting + registry affiliate integrations
- **Platforms:** iOS, Android, Web
- **Pricing:** Free (ad-supported); Evite Pro Plus ~$3-8/mo; Evite Premium event fees $10-30 per event
- **Distinguishing UX:** breadth of licensed invitation artwork; 26-year brand recognition

## How they make money (precise)
Primary: display ads on invitations and ecards (declining with premium push). Secondary: Evite Pro Plus subscriptions at ~$50-80/yr. Tertiary: affiliate revenue from partner gift-card platforms (Amazon, Starbucks), registry integrations, and group-gifting cuts. Unit economics are thin — ARPU est. <$0.20 across total user base; premium ARPU est. $50+.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **Ad-driven UX is dated:** Evite users see ads on their own invitations — a tell of declining model. MyLife has zero ads.
- **Dead social graph:** Evite predates smartphones; guest lists live in email silos. MyLife syncs to Friends module and contact history.
- **No event memory:** Evite events evaporate after the date. MyLife preserves RSVPs, guest preferences, expenses, and photos locally for future event planning.
- **Bundle economics:** At $3-8/mo standalone, Evite Pro Plus is 40-80% of MyLife's suite price — MyLife RSVP is one of 30 modules at comparable cost.
