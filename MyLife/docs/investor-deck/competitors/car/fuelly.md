# Fuelly — Deep Dive (vs MyCar)

**Module:** car
**Tier:** direct (fuel-economy niche)
**Founded:** 2008
**HQ:** USA (web-first)
**Status:** private (indie, acquired by SDC Publications)

## One-line
The OG mileage tracker — a web-first community that pre-dated mobile apps, still popular for MPG comparison and fuel logs.

## Product
- Fuel log with MPG calculation
- Service log
- Community MPG data by make/model/year
- Reminders
- Platforms: Web (primary), iOS, Android (basic)
- Pricing: free (ad-supported) / Premium ~$2.49/mo (ad-free + export)
- Distinguishing UX choice: public vehicle profiles (share your MPG with a community of owners)

## How they make money (precise)
1. **Display advertising** (Google AdSense + direct auto/parts brands).
2. **Premium subscription** (~$2.49/mo) removes ads, enables CSV export.
3. **Affiliate links** to auto-parts retailers.

Unit economics: negligible per-user hosting cost; ad RPM on automotive content ~$8-15 per 1,000 pageviews; declining as mobile traffic grew.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **Web-first legacy**: Fuelly's mobile apps are thin; MyCar is mobile-native.
- **Ads**: the free product is ad-supported — MyCar suite is ad-free.
- **Narrow scope**: Fuelly focuses on MPG; MyCar covers OBD, tires, insurance docs, GPS trips, parking.
- **Community data is public**: your vehicle's fill-ups are published on a URL. MyCar is private by default.
