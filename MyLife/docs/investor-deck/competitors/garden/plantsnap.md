# PlantSnap — Deep Dive (vs MyGarden)

**Module:** garden
**Tier:** direct
**Founded:** 2012 (launched 2017 as PlantSnap app)
**HQ:** Telluride, USA (PlantSnap, Inc.)
**Status:** private (early-mover plant-ID app; displaced by PictureThis/PlantIn)

## One-line
The first-to-market plant identifier (2017 TechCrunch darling), now a shrinking indie product outgunned by aggressive Asian/Eastern-European competitors on paid acquisition.

## Product
- Plant identification from photo
- Community photo-sharing feed (distinctive; competitors stripped this)
- Geolocation tagging of plant finds
- Plant encyclopedia
- Platforms: iOS, Android, Web
- Pricing: free tier + $19.99/yr Premium
- Distinguishing UX: community + "discover nearby plants" map that PictureThis abandoned

## How they make money (precise)
1. **Premium subscription** $19.99/yr — unlocks higher-accuracy identification, unlimited IDs, ad removal.
2. **Legacy one-time "lifetime" purchase** (sunset but honored).
3. Marginal ad-supported free tier.

Unit economics: constrained by inability to match PictureThis paid-acquisition spend.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **Community feed without privacy:** PlantSnap's community upload-every-plant design is a privacy anti-pattern. MyGarden keeps plants private by default.
- **Outgunned on acquisition:** PlantSnap has no Meta/TikTok ad machine; MyGarden doesn't need one because distribution comes via MyLife suite (30 other modules).
- **Shallow feature set:** ID + encyclopedia only; no companion planting, no frost dates, no harvest analytics, no layout planner, no propagation, no light meter.
- **Stagnant development:** App Store release cadence slowed post-2022; MyGarden ships monthly.
- **Geolocation leak:** PlantSnap geotags plant photos. MyGarden strips EXIF and keeps zone data coarse-grained.
