# Monarch Money — Deep Dive (vs MyBudget)

**Module:** budget
**Tier:** direct
**Founded:** 2018
**HQ:** San Francisco, USA
**Status:** private (VC-backed)

## One-line
The fastest-growing Mint successor; all-accounts aggregator plus budgeting plus investment tracking with polished design and aggressive VC growth.

## Product
- Bank + credit + investment aggregation via Plaid + Finicity
- Zero-based and goal-based budgeting
- Net worth tracking with custom asset categories
- Investment holdings + allocation view
- Shared households (multi-user)
- Platforms: iOS, Android, Web
- Pricing: $14.99/mo or $99.99/yr; 7-day free trial
- Distinguishing UX choice: unified net-worth + cashflow dashboard that feels like a private-bank client portal

## How they make money (precise)
1. **Subscriptions** ($14.99/mo or $99.99/yr): unlimited accounts, unlimited collaborators, full feature set.
2. **Family/household plan** (same price for household seats): encourages shared use.
3. No ads, no affiliate offers from partner banks (as of 2025 public statements).

Unit economics: Plaid + Finicity data fees run ~$0.50-$2/user/month, compressing margins; CAC heavily subsidized by VC. ARPU ~$90/yr, payback 12-18 months under aggressive-marketing regime.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **VC clock**: $850M post-money at ~$50M ARR = 17x revenue multiple; future price hikes or ad insertion likely. MyLife suite is bootstrapped + flat-priced.
- **Cloud-only**: every transaction routes through Monarch's servers. MyBudget is local-first with optional Plaid add-on.
- **Single-category lock-in**: Monarch is finance-only; MyLife suite crosses into health, creativity, academic tracking at the same price point.
- **Enshittification trajectory**: post-Mint, category leaders have reliably added ads or upcharges once VC runway tightens (see: Mint → TurboTax upsells, Rocket Money → Rocket Mortgage).
