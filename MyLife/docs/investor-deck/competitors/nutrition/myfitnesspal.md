# MyFitnessPal — Deep Dive (vs MyNutrition)

**Module:** nutrition
**Tier:** direct (category leader by users and revenue)
**Founded:** 2005
**HQ:** Austin, TX (under Francisco Partners)
**Status:** acquired twice (Under Armour 2015 $475M → Francisco Partners 2020 $345M)

## One-line
The category leader for food logging and calorie counting by volume; owns the largest food database and the most hated paywall in consumer fitness.

## Product
- Food logging with 18M+ food database (largest crowdsourced DB in the category)
- Barcode scanner, photo logging (Cal AI integration after Mar 2026 acquisition)
- Macro + micro tracking, recipe imports, meal planning (post-Intent 2025 acquisition)
- Premium features: custom macro goals, food analysis, remove ads, chart detail
- Connects to Apple Health, Google Fit, Fitbit, Garmin, Strava
- Platforms: iOS, Android, Web
- Pricing: Free (ad-supported, core logging) / Premium $19.99/mo or $79.99/yr / Premium+ $24.99/mo with AI
- Distinguishing UX choice: a food database too big to compete with, barcode-to-log in 3 seconds

## Acquisition / Exit
- Acquired by: Francisco Partners (PE, San Francisco/London)
- Date: 2020-11 (closed; originally announced Oct 2020)
- Price: $345M cash (Under Armour sold at $130M loss vs 2015 purchase)
- Current status inside parent: standalone portfolio company, still MyFitnessPal-branded
- Strategic rationale: classic PE buyout of distressed consumer-subscription asset with known paywall levers (recipes, barcode, macros) and 200M+ registered user top of funnel
- Founder outcome: Mike and Albert Lee exited at 2015 UA acquisition; no involvement in Francisco Partners era
- Prior exit: Under Armour $475M cash acquisition (Feb 2015); divested for $345M to FP in Nov 2020

## How they make money (precise)
1. **Premium ($19.99/mo or $79.99/yr)**: remove ads, custom macros, detailed food analysis, recipe importer, barcode export, exercise calorie adjustment.
2. **Premium+** (rolled out 2025, ~$24.99/mo): includes AI photo logging (Cal AI), coach features.
3. **In-app display ads** (free tier, significant share).
4. **B2B data API**: nutrition database licensing to other apps/brands (small but high-margin).

Unit economics: gross margin 85%+ on subs; ad RPM high (health/fitness intent). Francisco Partners focused on lifting conversion by aggressively paywalling recipe importer and macro customization in 2022-2023 — the playbook that drove user backlash and the migration to MacroFactor + Cronometer + Yazio.

## Wedge MyLife can exploit
- **User-trust rupture:** 2022 paywall of basic features (barcode export, recipes) created a migration window. MyNutrition ships all of them free on day one.
- **Ads in free tier:** MyFitnessPal is ad-heavy; MyLife is zero-ad.
- **Single-purpose silo:** MFP tracks food only. MyNutrition correlates food with MyFast (eating windows), MyWorkouts (protein timing), MyMood (diet-mood correlation).
- **PE ownership pressure:** Francisco Partners needs margin expansion — expect more paywalls. MyLife is consumer-owned flat subscription.
- **US-only focus:** MFP database is strong in US foods, weaker internationally. MyNutrition unifies USDA + Open Food Facts + FatSecret across regions.
