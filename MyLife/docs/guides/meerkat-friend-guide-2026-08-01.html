    • 1What Meerkat is

    Meerkat looks a lot like Discord. There are communities (Discord calls them servers) with channels inside, plus direct messages with friends. You will feel at home in about thirty seconds.

    What is different is underneath. There is no company holding your messages. Your account is a key created on your device that never leaves it. No email, no password, no sign-up. Messages travel between the people in the conversation, encrypted the whole way. Your phone is the home of your data. The app on your phone stores everything you write and everything shared with you; the website version is another window into the same world.

    ℹ️ How messages travel (worth 20 seconds)
    When you send a message, your device seals it and hands it to the connection server, which holds it for the other person (it can only see scrambled data, never the words). While their app is open, it arrives on its own within a few seconds. If their app is closed, the message waits and arrives when they next open it. You do not both need to be online at the same moment. Two things still use the manual Sync button: the very first sync with a new person (step 9) and moving big files. And because there are no push notifications yet, a closed app stays quiet; nothing arrives until it is opened.

    2The four things Trey sends you

    A
    A TestFlight invite (for your iPhone)

    An email or link from Apple that installs the real Meerkat app. If Trey has not sent this yet, use the web link below instead; everything in this guide works the same way.

    B
    A web link (for your computer, and as an iPhone fallback)

    A https address. It is temporary and points at Trey's machine, so if it stops loading one day, ask him for a fresh link before assuming something is broken.

    C
    A connection server address

    Starts with wss://. You will paste this once on every device in step 7. It is how your devices find each other across the internet.

    D
    A community invite link

    Starts with meerkat://community/join# and looks like nonsense. That is fine; the community itself is packed inside it.

    3Put it on your iPhone

    Path A: TestFlight (the real app)

    1
    Install TestFlight

    It is Apple's free app for testing apps before they are in the App Store. Get it from the App Store like any other app.

    2
    Open Trey's invite

    Tap the link in the invite email (or enter the code TestFlight shows you). Meerkat appears in TestFlight with an Install button.

    3
    Install and open Meerkat

    A 🦫 icon appears on your home screen. TestFlight may warn the beta can have bugs. Correct, that is why you are here.

    Path B: the web link (works today, no TestFlight needed)

    1
    Open the link in Safari

    It has to be Safari. Chrome on iPhone cannot add apps to the home screen. Links tapped from a message usually open in Safari already.

    2
    Tap the Share button

    The square with an arrow pointing up ↑ at the bottom of the screen.

    3
    Scroll down and tap "Add to Home Screen", then Add

    You may need to scroll the grey list quite far.

    4
    Open Meerkat from your home screen from now on

    It behaves like a normal app: its own icon, full screen, no browser bars.

    4Put it on your computer

    1
    Open the web link in Chrome or Edge

    Both work. Firefox works too but cannot install it as an app.

    2
    Click the install icon in the address bar

    A small screen-with-arrow icon or ⊕ at the right end of the address bar, then Install. If you cannot find it: the ⋮ menu at top right, then "Cast, save and share", then "Install page as app" (on Edge: Apps, then "Install this site as an app").

    3
    It opens in its own window

    No tabs, no address bar. It will also be in your Start menu or dock.

    5Important: every device is its own identity

    ⚠️ This is the biggest difference from Discord

    In Discord you log in with one account everywhere and every device is "you". Meerkat does not work that way. Each device creates its own key the first time you open it. Your phone and your computer start out as two separate identities with two separate safety codes.

    Do not try to copy one identity onto several devices. Set each device up separately, give each a name you will recognise (like Dan iPhone and Dan PC), and later use step 12 to link them so they count as one person. That is the supported way, and telling us how it feels is one of the most useful things you can do.

    6First run: set yourself up

    Do this on each device. It takes about a minute.

    A
    Answer the age question

    Type your birth month, day and year. It is checked on your device and thrown away. It is never sent anywhere and never saved.

    B
    Pick a name and write down your safety code

    Any name; it is just a label. Underneath it is a short safety code like 942b584e. That one is real: it comes from the key on your device and nobody else can have it. Screenshot it. You and Trey will read your codes to each other later to prove you are really talking to each other.

    C
    Choose "Just look around"

    You will join Trey's community properly in step 8. This just gets you in.

    D
    Unlock the app

    Meerkat costs $4.99 once, and until it is unlocked you cannot create or join anything. You will not pay anything as a tester. On the TestFlight app, tap Unlock: TestFlight uses Apple's test till, so the purchase goes through without charging your card. On the web test version, go to Settings (the ⚙ gear), find Unlock Meerkat, and press Restore purchase; the test server hands the unlock out for free. Trey will tell you which of the two applies. You know it worked when more icons appear down the left side.

    7Point it at the connection server

    Do this on every device before you try to send anything. Fifteen seconds, and it prevents the most annoying bug in the current build.

    1
    Settings, then Connection server

    Scroll to the section headed Connection server.

    2
    Click "I have a server URL" and paste the address from Trey

    It must start with wss:// (if Trey's address starts with https, change that beginning to wss). Press Save. It should say reachable.

    ℹ️ Why this step exists
    If you skip it you can end up with "No relay URL configured" when you try to sync, even when the screen above says the free server is reachable. Setting the address yourself avoids that completely. It is a known quirk; you do not need to report it.

    8Join Trey's community

    1
    Copy the invite link Trey sent

    The long meerkat://community/join#… one.

    2
    Press the "+" button

    Down the left-hand strip of icons. It opens Add a community.

    3
    Scroll to "Join with an invite link", paste, press Preview invite
    4
    Check the preview, then press Join community

    You should see the community name, member and channel counts, that you were invited by an owner, and when the invite expires. If any of that looks wrong, stop and tell Trey. Otherwise join; it appears in your left-hand strip.

    9Pair with Trey (one time)

    ⚠️ Joining the community is not enough
    In Discord, joining a server means you can talk. In Meerkat you also pair with the other person once by swapping a code. After that you never do it again with them. This is the step people get stuck on, so tell us honestly how confusing it felt.
    1
    Open Sync

    The Sync button at the top right of a channel.

    2
    Copy your pairing code and send it to Trey

    Under Pairing there is a long code starting MKPAIR1-. Press Copy code and send it any way you like: text, email, Discord. It is not secret.

    3
    Paste his code into your box and press Pair device

    He sends you his code; it goes in "Paste the other device's pairing code". Both of you should now see Paired devices: 1.

    4
    Read your safety codes to each other

    The short codes from step 6B. Say them on a call or send them by a different route than the pairing codes. If they match what each of you sees in the app, you are definitely talking to each other and not to somebody in the middle.

    10Send a message

    1
    Type a message and send it

    It appears on your screen right away and is handed to the connection server for Trey.

    2
    Watch it arrive on its own

    If Trey's app is open, the message should appear on his screen within a few seconds, with nobody pressing anything. If his app is closed, it arrives when he next opens it. Timing how long this actually takes, and whether it ever fails, is one of the most useful things you can test.

    ℹ️ If a message does not show up: the manual fallback
    Both of you open Sync and scroll to Manual session. Type the same phrase on both devices (at least 16 characters, exactly the same, for example testing-meerkat-monday). One presses Listen, the other presses Sync now, within a minute of each other. Under Recent sessions you should see a line like completed · wan_relay · sent 2 / received 0, and the messages appear. This manual session is also what moves big files, so it is worth trying once even if everything arrives on its own. There is also an Automatic connections switch in the Sync panel that dials your paired people every time you come back to the app.
    ✅ The manual flow is verified working
    We tested the manual session between two separate accounts and the messages went across. If neither the automatic arrival nor the manual session works for you, something is genuinely wrong and it is worth reporting.

    11Voice and video calls

    1
    Both of you open the app at an agreed time

    There are no push notifications yet, so a call cannot wake a closed app.

    2
    Open Messages, start a chat with Trey, press ☎ for voice or 📹 for video

    When asked for microphone (and camera) permission, press Allow. If you press Block the call cannot start and you will have to re-allow it in settings.

    3
    Answer, talk, then hang up and check the call log

    Try muting, unmuting, camera on and off. Messages has a Calls button; the call should be listed with the right direction and outcome.

    ⚠️ The "Room" button is a different thing
    At the top of a channel there is a Room button for a group voice room. That genuinely does not work yet because it needs a media server we have not deployed. Expect it to tell you so. One-to-one calls from Messages are the ones to test.

    12Make your phone and computer count as one person

    Without this step, your iPhone and your computer look like two different strangers to everyone else.

    1
    Pair your two devices with each other first

    Same as step 9, but between your own devices.

    2
    On the computer: Messages, find the linked-devices panel, press "Link this second device" next to your iPhone

    It will say they show as one person once the other device confirms. That is not a delay bug: your iPhone genuinely has to agree, because no device can quietly claim another.

    3
    Open Meerkat on the iPhone, let it sync, then check

    In a community you are both in, the member list should now show one row for you with something like "2 devices" under your name. Send a message from the iPhone; on the computer it should read as you, not as some other member.

    13When something breaks

    Something will break; that is the point of testing. When it does, send Trey three things:

    1
    A screenshot of what you see.
    2
    What you pressed right before it happened, best-guess is fine.
    3
    Which device (iPhone app, iPhone home-screen web app, or computer) and roughly what time.

    Known things you do not need to report: nothing arriving while the app is closed (there are no push notifications yet), the Room button not working, the web link changing when Trey restarts the test server, and the app asking you to re-enter the connection server address after that happens.
