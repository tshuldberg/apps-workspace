Testing Meerkat

A guide for a first-time tester. You do not need to know anything technical. If you can use Discord, you can do all of this.

What Meerkat is

Meerkat looks a lot like Discord. There are communities (Discord calls them servers), each with channels inside them, plus direct messages with friends. You will feel at home in about thirty seconds.

What is different is underneath. There is no company holding your messages. Your account is a key that is created on your device and never leaves it. There is no email, no password, and no sign-up. Messages travel directly between the people in the conversation, encrypted the whole way.

⚠️ The one thing that will surprise you

Messages do not arrive on their own yet, the way they do in Discord. After you send something, you press Sync, and the other person presses Listen, and then the messages move across. Both people need the app open at the same time.

This is not you doing it wrong. It is genuinely how the app works right now, and it is one of the main things we want your honest reaction to. Automatic syncing exists as a setting you can switch on, but the manual version is what we want tested first.

⚠️ Important: every device is its own identity

This is the biggest difference from Discord, and it changes how you set up. In Discord you log in with one account on your phone, your MacBook and your PC, and all three are "you". Meerkat does not work that way.

Each device generates its own key the first time you open it. Your phone, your MacBook and your PC are three separate identities with three separate safety codes. There is no login that carries an account between them.

Do not try to copy one identity onto several devices. There is a "Restore identity" feature that rebuilds the same identity from a backup, but it is for replacing a lost device, not for running the same identity in three places at once. Two live devices sharing one identity compete for the same inbox on the server, and whichever connects first takes the messages permanently. The others never see them.

So: set each device up separately, give each one a name you will recognise (Dan iPhone, Dan MacBook, Dan PC), and then link them together with Own devices on the Messages screen. That is the supported way to have your things on more than one device, and telling us how that actually feels is one of the most useful things you can do.

7Pair with Trey

⚠️ Joining the community is not enough

In Discord, joining a server means you can talk. In Meerkat you also have to pair with the other person once, by swapping a code. After that you never do it again with them. This is the step people get stuck on, so we want to know how confusing it felt.

1

Open Sync

The Sync button at the top right of a channel.

2

Copy your pairing code and send it to Trey

Under Pairing there is a long code starting MKPAIR1-. Press Copy code and send it to him any way you like: text, Discord, email. It is not secret.

3

Paste his code into your box

He sends you his. Paste it into "Paste the other device's pairing code" and press Pair device.

Both of you should now see Paired devices: 1.

4

Read your safety codes to each other

The short code from step 4B. Say yours out loud on a call, or send it separately. If they match what each of you sees in the app, you are definitely talking to each other and not to somebody in the middle.

8Actually send a message

1

Type your message and send it

It appears on your screen right away. It has not reached Trey yet. You will see something like "Waiting · 1 pending".

2

Both of you open Sync

Scroll down to Manual session.

3

Type the same phrase on both devices

Anything, as long as it is at least 16 characters and you both type it exactly the same. Agree it beforehand, for example testing-meerkat-monday.

4

One presses Listen, the other presses Sync now

It does not matter who does which. Do them within a minute of each other.

Under Recent sessions you should see a line like:

completed · wan_relay · sent 2 / received 0

Then check the channel. The other person's messages should be there.

✅ This is verified working

We tested exactly this between two separate accounts and the message went across correctly. If it does not work for you, something is genuinely wrong and it is worth reporting.

ℹ️ Want to try the hands-off version?

In the Sync panel there is Automatic connections. Switch it on and Meerkat will dial the people you are paired with by itself, every time you come back to the app. It only works while the app is open. Try it and tell us whether it makes the whole thing feel normal.

9Voice and video calls

Once you are paired with someone, you can call them. Open Messages, start a chat with them, and use the ☎ and 📹 buttons at the top of the conversation.

1

Both of you need the app open

There are no push notifications yet, so a call cannot wake a closed app. Agree a time, both open Meerkat, then call.

2

Press ☎ for voice or 📹 for video

Your browser will ask for permission to use your microphone (and camera for video). Press Allow. If you press Block, the call cannot start, and you will have to re-allow it in your browser settings.

On iPhone this only works from the home-screen app or Safari, and only over the https link. It will not work over a plain http address.

3

The other person answers

They should see the call arrive and be able to accept or decline. Once connected, try muting, unmuting, turning the camera on and off, and switching between front and back camera on a phone.

4

Hang up and check the call log

Messages has a Calls button. The call should be listed there with the right direction and outcome, including calls that were declined or missed.

ℹ️ What we know and what we do not

We have confirmed in the code and in the app that the call buttons are live for a paired person, that Meerkat uses your browser's real video calling, and that the signalling goes over the same connection server as everything else.

What we have not confirmed is a call actually connecting between two people, because that needs a human to press Allow on the microphone prompt. You two are the first to try it. Whatever happens, that is the result we need: it connecting, it failing, or it hanging.

⚠️ The "Room" button is a different thing

At the top of a channel there is a Room button for a group voice room. That one genuinely does not work yet, because it needs a media server we have not deployed. Expect it to tell you so. One-to-one calls from Messages are the ones to test.

10Your phone and your PC as one person

Meerkat gives every device its own identity, so without this step your iPhone and your PC look like two different strangers to everyone else. This step tells Meerkat they are both you.

Link them

  1. Pair the two devices first (that is step 7 above). Linking only offers devices you have already paired.
  2. On your PC, open Messages, find the linked-devices panel, and press Link this second device next to your iPhone.
  3. Read what it tells you. It should say they will show as one person once that device confirms. That is not a delay bug: your iPhone genuinely has to agree, because no device can quietly claim another.
  4. Open Meerkat on the iPhone and let it sync. Then go back to the PC.

Check it worked

  1. In a community you are both in, open the member list. You should now see one row for you, not two, with something like "2 devices" under your name.
  2. Press Connected devices on that row. It should list both devices separately, each with its own short code. Nothing is hidden; they are just tidied up by default.
  3. Send a message from the iPhone, then look at the PC. It should read as you, not as some other member.

Choose a different name in one community

  1. When you join a community you are asked for your name in this community. Try joining one with a nickname rather than your real name.
  2. Open that community's settings and change the nickname. It should save and stick.
  3. Check the other device: it should use the same nickname there. Both devices show the same name in that community, and your other communities are unaffected.

Pretend you lost a device

  1. Back in the linked-devices panel, press Remove this device next to one of them.
  2. Read the confirmation carefully and tell us whether it makes sense. It should say the device stops counting as yours, that a new key is generated which it does not have, and that anything already on it stays on it.
  3. Afterwards the member list should show you as one device again.
  4. You can link it back afterwards.

What to tell us. Did anything claim your devices were joined before the second one confirmed? Did any screen still show you as two people after linking? Did the removal wording make you feel confident about what actually happened to the lost device? Those three are exactly what we need to hear about.

11Add a friend in person (two iPhones, no internet)

This is the newest flow, and it is the one we most need real-device evidence for. Two people stand together, both press one button, hold their phones near each other, and confirm the same five emoji. No server, no internet, no codes to copy. It needs the full app build (TestFlight) on BOTH iPhones; the preview build says so honestly instead of showing the button.

The happy path

  1. On both iPhones, open Messages, press + to open Add friend, and press Add in person.
  2. Hold the phones near each other and keep both screens open. Within a few seconds each phone should show Confirm Adding [the other person's name] and five emoji.
  3. Compare the five emoji OUT LOUD. They must be exactly the same on both screens. That comparison is the security check; it is never optional.
  4. Press Confirm on both phones. Both should report success, and the new friend appears in Messages. Press Message and send a DM.

Prove it needs no internet

  1. On both iPhones: turn on Airplane Mode, then from Control Center turn Wi-Fi and Bluetooth back on (leave cellular off).
  2. Run the same flow again with a different pair of test identities, or cancel at the confirm screen if you are already friends. It should find the other phone and reach the emoji screen with no internet at all.

Try to break it (tell us what the copy said)

  1. Start a ceremony on both phones, then press Cancel on one. Both sides should end plainly, and both should say nothing was saved.
  2. Start one and let it sit. After 90 seconds it should time out on its own, again saving nothing.
  3. Start one and switch away from Meerkat mid-search. Coming back, the ceremony should have ended without saving anything.
  4. If the two screens EVER show different emoji, cancel and tell us immediately. That is the one report we want most.

Pair your own two devices the same way

  1. On the Sync screen's Pairing section, press Pair in person on both of your own devices instead of copying a pairing code.
  2. Afterwards the device should appear under Pairing exactly as a pasted code would have produced.

Check nearby sync recovers afterwards

  1. The screen says nearby device sync is paused while it searches. After the ceremony ends, run a normal nearby/local sync session between already-paired devices and confirm it still connects.
  2. If nearby sync stays broken until you restart the app, that is a bug we specifically asked this sweep to catch. Tell us.

What to tell us. Did the emoji match every time? Did every cancelled, timed-out, or interrupted ceremony say plainly that nothing was saved? Did nearby sync keep working after a ceremony? And did the whole thing feel like something you would show a friend?

13How to report something

Send Trey a short note. The more of this you can include, the better, but do not let missing details stop you from reporting:

  • What device: iPhone or PC, and which browser if you did not install it
  • What you were doing: "I pressed Sync now after typing the phrase"
  • What you expected: "his message should have appeared"
  • What actually happened: exact wording of any error, or a screenshot
  • Does it happen every time, or did it happen once?

✅ The most valuable thing you can tell us

Not the crashes. The moments where you did not know what to do next. Where you expected something to happen and it did not. Where a word made no sense. Where you thought "why do I have to do this?"

You are testing whether this can replace Discord for a group of friends. If it feels like homework, that is the single most important finding, and it is worth more than a bug report.
