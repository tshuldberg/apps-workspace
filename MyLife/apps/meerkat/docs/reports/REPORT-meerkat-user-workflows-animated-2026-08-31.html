How a person actually moves through Meerkat on each surface: the Expo app, the web app in a phone browser, and the web app on a Mac. Every screen below mirrors the real routes, tab labels, and status copy in the codebase as of commit 6ed8ce97.

Transport honesty, visualized. Nothing in these animations shows a faked state: DM badges appear only after a real sync round applies verified envelopes, "Delivered" and "Read by 1" come only from verified protocol receipts, community unread counts come from locally applied signed events, and public participation requires the one-time anonymous "Verify I'm human" check (a blind-signed credential that is never linked to your Meerkat identity).

2 Messages tabChats list with segments Chats / People / Calls. Maya's row is unread with a count of 2, driven by local read-state rows, not a server flag.
3 Open the threadThe DM thread renders on the shared chat kit. Every bubble is a locally verified signed event; the header shows the safety-code-checked state for this contact.
4 Write a replyComposer in the same props-only chat kit used by channels and post threads.
5 SendThe status line walks Sending… → Sent → Delivered. "Delivered" (and "Read by 1") appear only when a real verified receipt row exists, never optimistically.
dm_ tables are device-local and never replicate; delivery travels as end-to-end encrypted envelopes over a sync session (relay, LAN, or WebRTC rung).

1 Communities tabTwo communities carry unread counts. Each count is the number of verified signed channel events applied since your last read row, nothing is fetched from a server feed.
2 Open Trail CrewThe community home lists channels and Posts. #trip-planning shows 3 new; the post "Saturday sunrise hike?" has fresh replies. The community can carry its own owner-signed theme, verified before it renders.
3 Read the post threadThe post and its replies render on the same shared chat kit as DMs. The header shows exactly one audience label: "Shared with Trail Crew members."
4 React + replyTapping 🌲 writes a real signed react event (3 → 4, the chip is the recorded row, not an optimistic fake). Then a reply is typed.
5 SendThe reply is recorded locally and signed. Copy stays honest: it says "Sent", and members receive it when a sync session next connects, no claim that anyone saw it yet.
cm_ events are Ed25519-signed and verified at apply and at read; an unverified event renders nothing. Read state (cm_read_state) is personal and never sent over a shared community session.

1 Open your linkAny phone browser works. Type your Meerkat address, or tap the bookmark or home-screen icon you saved. The link points at your own hosted meerkat-web instance, so your data never lives at some company's site.
2 It checks, then unlocksThe app loads and shows the real gate: "Checking your purchase…", verifying the saved unlock with the connection server. Your identity and data already sit in this browser's encrypted vault as a paired device; nothing is fetched from an account. If it cannot verify, it says so, and Public + Discover stay free.
3 Land in your dataFeed opens with your communities' latest. A sync round runs, and the bottom nav (Feed · Communities · Public · Messages) shows a real Messages badge, the same honest pipeline as the app.
4 Reply to the DMSame thread, same chat kit behavior: Sending… → Sent → Delivered off a real receipt. This browser session is a paired device of your own identity, so both devices converge on the same conversation.
5 Respond in the community postReact and reply in the #trip-planning post thread. Web and app are verbatim twins here, the parity gate locks the exact strings and states across both surfaces.
6 Comment on the public postThe Public reader works the same in the browser: anonymous reading, one-time human check, then the comment posts under the blind-signed credential.
One codebase serves both browser workflows: meerkat-web collapses to a single-pane layout with MobilePrimaryNav under 860px and expands to the multi-pane shell on desktop.

1 Open the tabThe desktop shell: a community rail on the far left, a navigation column (Feed · Communities · Public · Messages), and content panes. After the vault unlocks and a sync round applies envelopes, Messages carries a real badge.
2 Reply to the DMDesktop gets the two-pane Messages view, chat list beside the open thread, with the identical Sending… → Sent → Delivered receipt behavior.
3 Respond in the communitySelecting Trail Crew in the rail swaps the nav column to its channels. The post thread, reactions, and audience label are string-for-string identical with the app (parity-locked).
4 Comment on the public postThe public reader in a wide layout: topics beside the open thread. Same anonymous-read, verify-once, comment-anonymously flow as every other surface.
The keyboard-friendly desktop shell (shortcuts, panes) and the phone layouts are one codebase; check-meerkat-parity.mjs locks shared state and pill strings across app and web so the surfaces cannot drift.
