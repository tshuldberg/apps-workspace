Boundary before conclusions

What this report does

This is a transcript-grounded argument inventory, not a fact-check. It records and organizes claims made by participants. Inclusion does not imply that a claim is true, well-supported, representative of everyone on a political side, or endorsed by Jubilee or the researcher.

Not a verdict on truthThis edition answers: “What arguments and counters appeared?” The next research phase should answer: “Which claims survive primary-source verification, where do they overreach, and what remains uncertain?”
  • Date window: 2025-09-02 through 2026-09-02, inclusive.
  • Discovery boundary: every upload in Jubilee’s official Surrounded playlist during the window, unioned with Jubilee channel uploads whose titles identify them as Surrounded reactions or follow-ups.
  • Corpus: 39 uploads, of which 27 are official-playlist entries and 12 are channel-title companions not present in that playlist.
  • Coverage: English automatic captions were obtained for all 39 uploads and processed in full, totaling 661,484 cleaned words and 54h 13m of video.
  • Argument boundary: 33 uploads supplied political, civic, identity, or cross-ideological material. Six health, longevity, veganism, or body-positivity uploads were screened but not forced into left/right categories. One compilation reused older excerpts and is not counted as independent support. This leaves 32 included, non-compilation discussion uploads.
  • “All arguments” means all distinct, generalizable proposition-and-reason families found in the corpus after consolidating repetition. Ads, host mechanics, applause, insults, personal biography with no general claim, and repeated clips were not counted as new arguments.
  • Automatic captions contain occasional names, punctuation, and homophone errors. The atlas paraphrases rather than reproducing transcripts. Timestamp links let a reader check the original exchange.

Across 54 hours

The recurring structure of disagreement

The same hidden conflicts recur under different topics. These patterns are the fastest route to a disciplined verification project.

01

Definitions carry the fight

Participants often appear to debate facts while using different definitions of fascism, corruption, violence, personhood, systemic racism, Zionism, safety, patriotism, or success.

02

The unit of analysis changes

One side moves from individual conduct to systems or group outcomes, while the other moves from group statistics to individual agency. Many rebuttals miss because they answer at a different level.

03

Time horizons conflict

Tariffs, industrial policy, public health, welfare, and crime arguments turn on whether current pain is evidence of failure or an investment whose result comes later.

04

Evidence standards are asymmetric

Speakers frequently demand direct proof for claims about their side while accepting patterns, motive, anecdotes, or institutional distrust as sufficient against the other.

05

Rights are rarely absolute in practice

Abortion, guns, speech, religion, borders, privacy, and protest involve collisions between claimed rights, duties, and risks. Saying “it is a right” begins rather than ends the analysis.

06

Institutional trust is the hidden variable

Disagreement over media, courts, agencies, medicine, universities, police, and elections shapes which evidence is admissible before policy is even discussed.

07

Labels flatten coalitions

Several episodes are establishment versus anti-establishment, nationalist versus noninterventionist, religious versus secular, or voter versus nonvoter, not clean Democratic versus Republican debates.

08

Common ground appears at the remedy level

Participants often share a concern such as elite impunity, unaffordable life, maternal support, political violence, bad policing, or lost trust, then divide over cause, scale, and enforcement.

ARG-001

What counts as authoritarianism?

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The progressive case defines authoritarianism behaviorally: denying a certified election, pressuring election officials, advancing alternate electors, directing prosecution toward named opponents, threatening media or universities, deploying state power against protest, and treating the opposition as illegitimate. The warning does not require a completed one-party dictatorship; erosion can be incremental.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The conservative reply uses a narrower threshold: the United States still has elections, courts, opposition parties, media criticism, and a presidential term limit, so analogies to historic fascist regimes are overstated. It adds that Democratic administrations also used agencies, mandates, platform pressure, and prosecutions against opponents, making selective condemnation partisan.

Verify nextSeparate legal acts, rhetoric, administrative pressure, and completed institutional change. Test each cited action rather than treating the label itself as proof.
ARG-002

The 2020 election and transfer of power

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left argues that losing court challenges, continuing to claim victory, pressuring officials to find votes, promoting alternate electors, and encouraging the January 6 crowd form one attempt to defeat the lawful result. A later departure from office does not erase the attempted disruption.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right counters that candidates may challenge irregularities in court, alternate-elector arrangements have historical analogues, Trump ultimately left office, and criminal theories around the episode were politically selected. Some participants distinguish peaceful protesters from rioters and say due-process failures justified pardons.

Verify nextCreate a dated event chain, identify the legal status of each act, and distinguish allegation, indictment, adjudication, pardon, and political characterization.
ARG-003

Prosecuting political figures

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives say a president publicly naming desired targets, replacing a reluctant prosecutor, and then obtaining indictments is strong evidence of politicized justice. Accountability should follow evidence through independent processes, including for Democrats, but direct presidential steering is the danger.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives answer that Letitia James, James Comey, and others may have committed chargeable offenses, while cases against Trump stretched statutes or legal theories. On this view, prosecuting real crimes is not authoritarian, and prior Democratic officials already used law enforcement politically.

Verify nextFor every case, document the charge, evidence, prosecutorial chain, judicial rulings, comparable cases, and any presidential communication. Do not infer innocence or guilt from party identity.
ARG-004

Mandate versus constraint

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left argues that an electoral mandate authorizes policy within law, not warrantless detention, punishment of disfavored speech, cancellation of constitutional rights, or defiance of courts. Rights exist partly to constrain temporary majorities.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says voters explicitly chose border enforcement, trade controls, institutional reform, and a break with the prior establishment. Bureaucratic resistance and aggressive litigation can frustrate democratic choice, so executive speed is framed as carrying out the mandate rather than seizing power.

Verify nextMap each disputed action to statutory authority, constitutional limits, emergency powers, judicial review, and actual voter-facing campaign language.
ARG-005

Living Constitution versus fixed restraint

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressive participants say the Constitution was designed to change, has repeatedly been amended to include excluded people, and must be interpreted in light of present rights. Some go further and argue for a new constitutional settlement that explicitly protects everyone.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservative and heterodox participants answer that a written Constitution exists precisely to keep passing majorities from redefining rights at will. If rival camps no longer accept shared meanings, court legitimacy and constitutional restraint weaken even if the paper document remains.

Verify nextDistinguish amendment, judicial interpretation, executive noncompliance, and wholesale replacement. These are different constitutional claims.

ARG-006

Counting violence

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left cites studies and law-enforcement assessments that attribute more lethal extremist violence to the right, especially white-supremacist and anti-government actors. It rejects treating every opportunistic theft or property crime during a mass protest as ideologically left-wing.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right counters that ordinary voters experience riots, arson, vandalism, attacks on businesses, threats on campuses, attacks on Tesla property, and celebration of political killings as a visible pattern associated with left rhetoric. It separates openly white-nationalist factions from the Republican mainstream.

Verify nextPredefine units: fatalities, injuries, property damage, plots, riots, or incidents. Code ideology and organizational ties consistently on both sides.
ARG-007

Institutional versus street violence

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives broaden the frame to institutional harm: aggressive immigration enforcement, deaths in custody, family separation, discriminatory policing, and overseas war are state violence supported by the right. Counting only private actors hides the side controlling government force.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right and heterodox response accepts that state violence can matter but notes that police departments in Democratic cities also use force against left protesters. It argues that attributing every agency action to a broad ideology can become as loose as attributing every rioter to Democrats.

Verify nextMaintain separate ledgers for private political violence, civil disorder, law-enforcement force, detention harm, and military action.
ARG-008

Does rhetoric cause attacks?

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says leaders calling opponents enemies, extremists, traitors, or people who should be punished normalizes violence from the top. It stresses that Democratic officials condemned attacks, while prominent right figures sometimes mocked victims or praised force.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right argues that repeated fascist, Nazi, existential-threat, or billionaire-enemy rhetoric supplies moral permission to unstable individuals. Participants point to attacks on Trump, Charlie Kirk, and executives, plus online celebration, as evidence of a cultural problem even without an elected official issuing a direct order.

Verify nextSeparate direct incitement, foreseeable stochastic risk, post-event celebration, and ordinary harsh criticism. Record speaker reach and exact wording.
ARG-009

January 6 versus 2020 unrest

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives treat January 6 as uniquely anti-democratic because violence occurred during certification of an election and served an effort to keep the losing president in power. Property damage during racial-justice protests, while punishable, did not seek to overturn a national vote.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives emphasize the scale and duration of 2020 riots, private livelihoods destroyed, and uneven prosecution. They distinguish nonviolent January 6 attendees from assailants, contest claims about instigation, and say partisan language erases misconduct by authorities.

Verify nextCompare purpose, duration, casualties, property loss, prosecutions, state response, and relationship to political leaders. Avoid a single moral-equivalence score.
ARG-010

Is civil war a serious forecast?

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives argue the forecast is sensational because the United States lacks the geographic fracture, military defection, and institutional collapse normally associated with civil war. Repetition can itself encourage armed fatalism and monetize fear.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The heterodox-right case points to polarized jurisdictions, interstate abortion disputes, federal-state border confrontations, divergent constitutional interpretations, and reciprocal beliefs that the other coalition is illegitimate. It presents conflict as a risk, not a desire.

Verify nextDefine civil war, insurgency, sustained political violence, and constitutional crisis separately. Build leading indicators before judging probability.

ARG-011

Government censorship versus private consequence

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left distinguishes the First Amendment from criticism, boycotts, moderation, and employment decisions by private actors. Speech does not create an entitlement to an audience, job, television slot, or immunity from other people speaking back.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right argues that loss of employment, coordinated platform bans, advertiser pressure, and government contacts with media companies can chill speech as effectively as formal law. When regulators threaten licenses or officials route requests through platforms, the private-state distinction can become artificial.

Verify nextFor each example, identify the actor, legal authority, communication, threatened penalty, platform rule, and whether a court found state action.
ARG-012

COVID-19 information controls

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives defend some interventions as responses to dangerous medical misinformation during an emergency, while still allowing that agencies and platforms made mistakes. Harm prevention, provenance labels, and moderation are not automatically bans on dissent.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives and anti-establishment participants say government requests to remove claims, changing guidance, mandates, and professional punishment converted contested science into enforced orthodoxy. Good intentions do not eliminate authoritarian methods.

Verify nextAudit requests and coercion separately, then test the accuracy and uncertainty available at the time rather than using hindsight alone.
ARG-013

Campus protest and immigration status

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left argues that arrest, detention, deportation pressure, grant threats, or visa consequences aimed at Palestine advocacy punish protected ideas. Disruptive conduct can be addressed without making political viewpoint the trigger.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right replies that protest does not include threats, occupation, harassment, support for prohibited groups, or shutting down other students. Noncitizen status and institutional funding may create legal questions beyond an ordinary citizen speaking on a sidewalk.

Verify nextSeparate protected speech, material support, trespass, threats, immigration authority, and funding conditions. Use adjudicated facts, not montage clips.
ARG-014

Cancel culture as harm

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The progressive counter says accountability and voluntary association are themselves speech rights. Public criticism is not fascism, and highly amplified examples can distract from wages, housing, health care, and concentrated ownership.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The conservative case says networked mobs impose disproportionate livelihood penalties for lawful opinions, creating ideological conformity without due process. The practical threat to ordinary people may be more immediate than formal prosecution.

Verify nextMeasure frequency, reversibility, process, power imbalance, and viewpoint symmetry. Separate criticism from coordinated economic punishment.
ARG-015

Media gatekeeping and selective coverage

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says weakly sourced claims can be delayed or contextualized without proving a coordinated cover-up. The Hunter Biden material yielded personal wrongdoing but, participants argued, did not establish criminal conduct by Joe Biden.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says delayed reporting, expert letters, platform restrictions, and reluctance to obtain contested evidence protect establishment figures. When institutions investigate themselves, absence of charges does not necessarily restore trust.

Verify nextCreate a publication timeline, authenticate each artifact, track what outlets knew, and distinguish unverified caution from suppression.

ARG-016

Capitalism as innovation engine or power structure

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left argues that innovation predates capitalism and also comes from public research, universities, workers, and open collaboration. Private ownership can convert useful invention into rent extraction, especially for necessities with weak consumer exit.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says profit, ownership, competition, and the possibility of unequal reward organize risk-taking and produce abundance better than known alternatives. Government allocation weakens information, incentives, and accountability.

Verify nextCompare sectors and institutional designs rather than treating capitalism or government as one uniform mechanism.
ARG-017

Positive and negative liberty

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives say formal freedom means little without health care, housing, education, time, and protection from unpayable debt. Public provision can expand real choice by reducing dependence on an employer, landlord, or wealthy benefactor.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives stress freedom from coercion: taxes and mandates seize the product of work, while promised positive rights require compelling someone else to provide them. Voluntary exchange and charity preserve agency.

Verify nextState the liberty concept being used, who bears the duty, and whether the proposed institution increases or displaces practical choice.
ARG-018

Taxing wealth and high incomes

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left argues that billionaires rely on public infrastructure, legal protections, labor, and publicly funded technology while often paying a lower effective rate than workers. Higher top rates and limits on avoidance can fund shared goods and reduce purchased political power.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right replies that high earners already pay much of the income-tax total, wealth is not the same as cash income, and punitive rates can move investment, firms, and tax bases. Asset taxes can reach pensions, businesses, or unrealized value and weaken job creation.

Verify nextCompare marginal, average, and effective rates; income versus wealth; incidence; avoidance; migration; revenue; and use of funds.
ARG-019

Inheritance and merit

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives argue that large inheritance contradicts meritocracy by assigning life chances through birth. It compounds ownership across generations and makes equal opportunity largely rhetorical.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives say caring for children is a central reason people work and save. Preventing parents from transferring property violates family autonomy and can punish long-term stewardship.

Verify nextModel thresholds and exemptions. Distinguish ordinary family transfers from dynastic concentrations.
ARG-020

Top versus bottom or left versus right

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The populist-left case says concentrated wealth finances both parties, algorithms monetize conflict, and culture disputes divert attention from monopoly, wages, housing, health costs, and campaign finance. Working people share more material interests with one another than with billionaire patrons.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right responds that ideology still shapes abortion, religion, borders, crime, speech, and family policy, so class cannot dissolve genuine moral conflict. Entrepreneurs are not a single hostile class, and some wealth reflects value creation rather than political capture.

Verify nextMap when donor interests converge across parties and when voter preferences independently explain policy.

ARG-021

Who pays a tariff?

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says importers pay at the border and frequently pass costs to consumers or downstream producers, making broad tariffs a regressive tax. Claims that foreign governments directly pay the Treasury obscure domestic incidence.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right acknowledges short-term consumer cost but frames it as leverage: foreign producers may cut margins, trading partners may remove barriers, and domestic production may replace imports. The relevant test is long-run national capacity, not the first price increase.

Verify nextTrace statutory payer, pass-through, exchange rates, margins, substitution, and sector-level consumer prices.
ARG-022

Blanket versus targeted protection

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives accept that narrow strategic protection can support semiconductors or security, but argue that unstable executive-order tariffs across allies and inputs deter investment and invite retaliation. A future president can reverse them before factories pay back.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives say dependence on geopolitical rivals and deindustrialization require broad bargaining pressure. Temporary discomfort is the cost of rebuilding supply chains and ending a trade regime that exported jobs for decades.

Verify nextCompare targeted and blanket policies on investment horizons, capacity created, retaliation, and security value.
ARG-023

Manufacturing outcomes

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left points to investment and employment indicators, project delays, and higher input prices to argue that rhetoric about reshoring has not yet produced a golden age. It contrasts durable legislative subsidies with reversible proclamations.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right cites announced plants, trade commitments, and future jobs, arguing that industrial projects take years and cannot be judged after months. It also says prior headline growth hid communities hollowed out by trade.

Verify nextDistinguish announcements, committed capital, construction, operating capacity, jobs, and net sector employment.
ARG-024

Farm losses and bailouts

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives say tariffs raise machinery and fertilizer costs, trigger retaliation, and then require taxpayer bailouts. Farmers often prefer restored markets to compensation for policy-created losses.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives say subsidies bridge a strategic transition and offset foreign retaliation, labor shocks, or distorted global markets. Temporary support does not refute the national-security case for trade reform.

Verify nextCalculate pre-policy baseline, export loss, input inflation, aid paid, beneficiaries, and whether market share returns.
ARG-025

Presidential accountability for prices

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left holds leaders to explicit promises on energy, groceries, gas, and immediate relief, especially when they claimed uniquely fast competence. Rising prices, unemployment, or weaker manufacturing contradict celebratory branding.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right argues presidents inherit inflation, debt, supply chains, and pandemic disruption, while structural repair takes longer than one year. Policy direction and future investment matter more than literal campaign timing.

Verify nextUse a dated promise ledger, inherited trend, implementation date, plausible lag, and counterfactual range.

ARG-026

Do benefit cuts affect only recipients?

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says reduced Medicaid and food aid shift cost rather than erase it: untreated illness reaches emergency rooms and premiums, rural providers close, hunger weakens learning and work, and lost food demand reaches farms and stores. Benefits are social infrastructure.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right argues that programs can contain fraud, ineligible enrollment, administrative bloat, and incentives for long-term dependency. Taxpayers and middle-income families also bear costs, so targeted eligibility and work expectations can protect the system.

Verify nextMeasure eligibility changes, direct savings, shifted state and private costs, provider closures, fraud recovered, and household outcomes.
ARG-027

Aid and personal responsibility

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives argue that people cannot bootstrap while hungry, sick, underpaid, or caring for children without support. Assistance can enable education, job search, entrepreneurship, and family stability rather than replacing effort.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives say durable progress depends on work, family, saving, community institutions, and expectations. Unconditional or poorly designed aid can displace those networks and trap recipients in benefit cliffs.

Verify nextTrack employment, earnings, health, family stability, program exit, and benefit cliffs by program design.
ARG-028

Government or community provision

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says charity is uneven, conditional, and incapable of guaranteeing access at national scale. Public programs create enforceable rights and spread risk across healthy and sick, rich and poor.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says local charities and community groups know needs better, can require accountability, and avoid remote bureaucracy. Government programs may crowd out voluntary provision and turn support into political leverage.

Verify nextCompare coverage, administrative cost, geographic consistency, responsiveness, stigma, and continuity under funding shocks.
ARG-029

Health insurance structure

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives argue that a public option or broader public coverage can remove insurer middlemen, improve bargaining power, and make preventive care available before crises. Existing program gains show reform can work even if incomplete.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives point to waiting, rationing, provider payment pressure, fraud, and government failure. Competition, price transparency, personal choice, and deregulation are offered as safer than placing more care under political control.

Verify nextCompare total cost, access, wait times, health outcomes, provider participation, choice, and financial risk.
ARG-030

Pro-life support after birth

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left challenges abortion opponents to support maternal care, paid leave, child care, nutrition, health insurance, and poverty reduction after birth. A culture of life is incomplete if legal protection ends at delivery.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right-side response in the Lila Rose follow-up accepts more support, including free birth, cash or child subsidies, adoption pathways, and pregnancy-resource centers, but prefers redirecting funds from abortion providers and retaining sexual responsibility as the first prevention strategy.

Verify nextInventory concrete funding, eligibility, provider standards, adoption safeguards, maternal outcomes, and whether proposed support matches projected need.

ARG-031

Enforcement priority

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left favors concentrating limited resources on violent offenders, trafficking, organized crime, and border operations. Broad workplace, neighborhood, or courthouse raids spend capacity on settled families and increase costly mistakes.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right argues that unauthorized presence is itself a legal category that cannot be ignored. Selective enforcement creates a semi-enforced system, disadvantages legal immigrants, and signals that law depends on political sympathy.

Verify nextSeparate removals by criminal conviction, pending charge, civil status, recent entry, final order, and mistake or release.
ARG-032

Safety and crime

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives say immigrants as a group are not established to offend at higher rates, and blanket enforcement may make communities less safe by diverting agents from violent targets and deterring victims or witnesses from calling police.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives cite murders, gangs, fentanyl, trafficking, repeat offenders, and preventable victims as evidence that weak enforcement imposes unacceptable risk. Even a low average rate does not answer why a removable offender remained.

Verify nextCompare rates and absolute cases, legal status definitions, offense severity, removal eligibility, enforcement opportunity, and reporting behavior.
ARG-033

Due process and enforcement methods

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left objects to masks, uncertain agency identification, warrant disputes, racial profiling, detention of citizens or lawful residents, transfers, poor detention conditions, and defiance of courts. Some participants propose replacing current ICE functions with transparent specialized agencies.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says dangerous operations require officer protection, immigration law already provides process, mistakes occur in every enforcement system, and abolishing an agency over misconduct sacrifices sovereignty. Reform bad practice without disabling enforcement.

Verify nextAudit warrants and authority by operation, identification rules, error rates, detention conditions, remedies, and compliance with court orders.
ARG-034

Jobs, wages, housing, and services

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left says immigrants work, pay taxes, start businesses, increase demand, and contribute more than simple benefit tallies imply. Housing inflation is driven primarily by underbuilding, finance, zoning, and corporate ownership, not a single migrant group.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right says rapid inflows expand labor supply, compete for entry-level work, strain schools and shelter, add housing demand, and shift costs to citizens and legal immigrants. Employers can use unauthorized labor to suppress wages.

Verify nextEstimate effects by skill, region, time horizon, fiscal level, housing supply response, labor substitution, and consumer demand.
ARG-035

Asylum, temporary status, and legalization

L Left or progressive argument and counter

Progressives distinguish asylum seekers and lawful temporary residents from people evading all process, and support a path for long-settled, tax-paying residents. Changing status rules to meet a removal quota is framed as manufacturing illegality.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

Conservatives say asylum and temporary programs were stretched beyond intended limits, creating years of de facto residence. A path that rewards unlawful entry is unfair to applicants waiting abroad and can invite another surge.

Verify nextDocument statutory status, processing delay, adjudication results, renewal authority, proposed eligibility, and future-flow incentives.
ARG-036

Economic and civic identity

L Left or progressive argument and counter

The left frames immigration as a continuing source of growth, entrepreneurship, demographic renewal, and American identity. Law-abiding neighbors share more interests with working citizens than either group shares with concentrated wealth.

R Right or conservative argument and counter

The right frames controlled admission as a prerequisite for solidarity: a country must know who is present, choose numbers it can absorb, enforce common rules, and prioritize the existing political community.

Verify nextTest economic claims separately from the normative question of membership. Neither resolves the other by itself.

Net benefit of police presence

The progressive counter says discriminatory stops, force, wrongful detention, criminalization of low-level conduct, and lifelong collateral consequences can create more harm than safety. Public safety should be measured by harm prevented, not arrests produced.

ARG-038

Bias and disparity
